**Bottom line:** Germany arrested a former chief of its foreign intelligence service on espionage charges and opened its first Nord Stream sabotage trial, while Washington quietly agreed to drop its spy case against former CIA analyst Sue Mi Terry weeks before trial. Russia kept up hybrid pressure on Europe, hitting Ukraine's power grid overnight and drawing Polish accusations that Moscow sabotaged a Starlink hub using hired foreign nationals. A Saudi national working at OpenAI was charged with bugging an executive conference room, and US authorities dismantled another slice of North Korea's fraudulent remote-IT-worker network.

## Espionage and counterintelligence

**FBI arrests Saudi national for allegedly bugging OpenAI's offices.** Abdulrahman Alfozan, 35, a Saudi national working as an OpenAI engineer, was charged with secretly planting a recording device in an executive conference room at the company's San Francisco office. OpenAI said it helped identify the device and fired Alfozan before the FBI made the arrest. *Why it matters:* The case raises the question of whether a foreign government was trying to collect intelligence on a company at the center of the AI race.
([The Record](https://therecord.media/saudi-national-charged-with-spy-device-openai))

**Germany arrests former BND chief on espionage charges, probes Mossad-linked ties.** German prosecutors arrested August Hanning, 80, who led the BND foreign intelligence service from 1998 to 2005, on suspicion of treasonous espionage, accusing him of taking roughly 2,000 classified documents from a former aide and using them in private consulting; investigators are also checking whether he passed material to China or Russia. The case grew out of a separate investigation into an attempted child abduction, in which the father alleges Hanning enlisted help from an Israeli cybersecurity firm chaired by former Mossad chief Danny Yatom, who denies any role; Hanning denies involvement in the abduction. *Why it matters:* A former chief of Germany's foreign intelligence service facing an espionage charge is a serious breach for an agency that handles allied secrets, however the case resolves.
([Ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/rk5ybegszx), [Ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/q8qzh5ufy))

**Russia detains Japanese journalist on spying accusations.** Russia's FSB security service detained Yuki Imai, a journalist with the Hokkaido Shimbun, accusing him of collecting information on Moscow's defense capabilities in the Kuril Islands for Tokyo. Japan has demanded fair treatment for Imai and summoned Russia's ambassador. *Why it matters:* The arrest lands amid already tense Japan-Russia relations over the Kurils and gives Moscow a potential bargaining chip.
([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cwp9g1el7x9zo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss))

**US agrees to drop South Korea spy case against former CIA analyst Sue Mi Terry.** Federal prosecutors in Manhattan entered a deferred prosecution agreement with Sue Mi Terry, a former CIA analyst and National Security Council official charged in 2024 with acting as an unregistered agent for South Korea; the charges will be dismissed in 12 months if she complies with travel and contact restrictions. Terry admitted accepting gifts and sharing notes with South Korean intelligence officers but denied wrongdoing. *Why it matters:* Ending the case weeks before trial, without prosecutors explaining why, is a striking reversal in one of the highest-profile foreign-agent cases brought against a US foreign policy figure in years.
([NK News](https://www.nknews.org/2026/10/us-to-drop-charges-against-sue-mi-terry-expert-accused-of-aiding-seoul-spies/), [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/world/20261014/us-to-drop-s-korea-intelligence-case-against-ex-cia-analyst))

**Justice Department charges 14 in North Korean "laptop farm" scheme, FBI seizes 187 devices.** The Justice Department announced coordinated charges against 14 people in a scheme that used stolen American identities to get North Korean IT workers remote jobs at more than 70 US companies, funneling roughly $3 million back to Pyongyang; FBI agents raided more than 40 residences in Texas and seized 187 devices. *Why it matters:* The scale, dozens of companies infiltrated over years, shows how deeply North Korea's remote-work fraud has penetrated US hiring pipelines to help fund its weapons programs.
([Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-coordinated-actions-combat-north-korean-remote-it-worker), [CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/doj-north-korea-remote-work-laptop-farms/), [The Record](https://therecord.media/north-korea-laptop-farms-worker-scams-doj-raids-texas))

**Germany opens first Nord Stream sabotage trial in Hamburg.** German prosecutors put a former Ukrainian military officer, identified as Serhii K, on trial in Hamburg on charges of helping coordinate the 2022 bombing of the Nord Stream gas pipelines, the first such trial over the attack four years on. *Why it matters:* A conviction would be the first time a court formally establishes criminal responsibility for one of the most consequential acts of sabotage against European infrastructure since the war began.
([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cw99zpmnxzmjo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss))

## US military and intelligence community

**Army warns budget impasse could delay HADES spy plane production.** The Army's intelligence chief, Maj. Gen. Andrew Evans, said a continuing resolution could "complicate" production of the HADES airborne signals-intelligence plane, which he said is currently on cost and schedule. *Why it matters:* A prolonged budget standoff threatens to slow a program meant to replace aging surveillance aircraft just as demand for persistent intelligence collection grows.
([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/continuing-resolution-could-complicate-armys-hades-spy-plane-production-official/))

**Army stands up new command for autonomous warfare.** The Army redesignated its Futures and Concepts Command as the Futures and Autonomous Systems Command, with a three-star general set to lead the new Autonomous Warfare Command focused initially on corps-level systems. *Why it matters:* Elevating autonomy to its own command signals the Army expects unmanned and AI-enabled systems to become central to how it fights, not a side project.
([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/news/your-military/2026/10/13/us-army-to-stand-up-command-for-unmanned-and-autonomous-warfare/), [Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/laneve-redesignates-armys-futures-and-concepts-command-as-new-fascom-autonomy-command/), [DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/10/13/three-star-will-head-armys-new-autonomy-command-which-will-initially-focus-on-corps-level-systems/))

**Army reassesses munitions buys as Iran war continues, prioritizing Patriot missiles.** The Army's fires acquisition chief said buying more Patriot interceptor missiles is now a top procurement priority as the service re-evaluates its munitions goals because of the ongoing war with Iran. *Why it matters:* Sustained conflict is forcing the Army to compete with allies for scarce air-defense interceptors, exposing how thin US stockpiles have become.
([Defense One](https://www.defenseone.com/defense-systems/2026/10/army-munitions-procurement-iran/416567/))

## China, Russia, Iran and North Korea

**Poland says Russia behind Starlink station sabotage, two held in Germany.** Poland's government said two Turkish nationals have been detained in Germany on suspicion of sabotaging a Starlink ground station near Warsaw on the instructions of a foreign intelligence service, and said it believes Russia was behind the September fire, which disrupted internet access for Ukraine's military. Special Services Minister Tomasz Siemoniak said "We have no doubt that Russian services are behind this act of sabotage." *Why it matters:* An attack on infrastructure carrying Ukrainian military traffic, carried out through hired foreign nationals, fits a pattern of Russia using proxies for sabotage across Europe to maintain deniability.
([Cyprus Mail](https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/10/12/poland-says-two-turks-detained-in-germany-over-starlink-fire-it-blames-on-russia))

**Russia pounds Ukraine's power grid overnight, kills at least six.** Russian strikes on Ukraine killed at least six people and damaged power stations overnight, as Ukraine says speed restrictions on Elon Musk's Starlink service are also disrupting battlefield operations. *Why it matters:* The strikes came just days after Trump announced a surprise energy "ceasefire," underlining how little the truce has actually changed on the ground.
([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cm2d6e62p49do?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss))

**China-linked hackers breach Fiji regional organization, US warns of wider targeting.** The US embassy in Suva said China-linked hackers breached a regional organization's network in Fiji between July and August, and warned that governments and telecom networks across the Pacific are being targeted. Beijing said it "firmly opposes hacking activities" and denied involvement. *Why it matters:* The warning adds to a string of suspected Chinese cyber intrusions against Pacific institutions, a region where Washington and Beijing are competing hard for influence.
([Hong Kong Free Press](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/10/13/china-linked-hackers-hit-fiji-us-embassy/))

## Germany and Israel

**Bundeswehr personnel tops 190,000 for first time since conscription suspension.** Defense Minister Boris Pistorius said the Bundeswehr's personnel strength passed 190,000 soldiers in October, the largest the force has been since Germany suspended compulsory military service in 2011.
([Augen geradeaus!, in German](https://augengeradeaus.net/2026/10/bundeswehr-personalstaerke-laut-pistorius-im-oktober-2026-ueber-190-000/))

**IDF says it killed Hamas commander holding Israeli hostages, in an apparent ceasefire breach.** The Israel Defense Forces (IDF) said it killed Momen Ahmad Salameh Ay, an aide to commanders of Hamas's Gaza Brigade whom it accused of helping hold Israeli hostages and planning new attacks on Israeli troops and civilians in violation of the ceasefire. *Why it matters:* A lethal Israeli strike inside Gaza raises questions about how durable the ceasefire actually is.
([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/gaza/2026-10-14/ty-article/.premium/idf-says-it-killed-hamas-commander-who-held-israeli-hostages-captive-in-gaza/000001a1-3911-d217-a5ab-7f55c5000000))

**Former Shin Bet chief says he will sue Netanyahu over October 7 accusations.** Ronen Bar, who led the Shin Bet internal security service until his dismissal, said he will file a defamation suit against Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu over claims that Bar withheld pre-attack warnings and failed to alert field forces on October 7. Bar says he warned a Knesset intelligence subcommittee of war risk weeks before the attack and has accused Netanyahu of selectively releasing documents; Netanyahu has filed his own libel suits over separate reports that he was warned in advance. *Why it matters:* The dueling lawsuits show Israel's fight over responsibility for the October 7 failures is now moving from public accusation into the courts.
([The Print](https://theprint.in/world/ex-chief-of-israels-internal-security-service-says-he-will-sue-netanyahu-over-7-october-accusations/3057677/), [Ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/i3cdyckhv))
