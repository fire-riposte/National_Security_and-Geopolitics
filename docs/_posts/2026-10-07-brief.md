**Bottom line:** A German court is deciding whether to keep former BND president August Hanning in custody after his arrest for treason and espionage, and German media now say the foreign service he allegedly served was not Russia's, with evidence tracing instead to a Danish kidnapping investigation involving former Israeli agents. The FBI arrested an Irvine, California real estate agent accused of surveilling Taiwan President Lai Ching-te's son for Chinese intelligence, part of a broad day of China-linked espionage cases that also saw Taiwan indict four people over Hong Kong-funded spying on a lawmaker and the UK warn universities to cut ties with a Chinese institute MI5 calls an espionage front. The EU also moved toward its largest-ever sanctions package against Russia's defense industry.

## Espionage and counterintelligence

**Germany's ex-spy chief awaits a detention ruling, and the foreign service he allegedly served wasn't Russia's.** A German court was set to rule on whether to keep former Bundesnachrichtendienst (BND) president August Hanning in custody following his arrest on suspicion of treason and espionage. German media reported the foreign intelligence service he allegedly served was not Russian, and that the evidence against him emerged from a separate investigation into the abduction of a German heiress's children in Denmark, an operation that reportedly involved former Israeli agents. *Why it matters:* The case keeps widening in a direction that cuts against the Russia narrative many assumed, which matters for how Germany recalibrates its counterintelligence priorities. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/former-german-spy-chief-detained-suspicion-espionage-treason-bild-reports-2026-10-06/), [DW](https://www.dw.com/en/germanys-ex-spy-chief-arrested-for-espionage/a-79560278), [Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/world-news/europe/2026-10-06/ty-article/germany-arrests-ex-chief-spy-on-espionage-and-treason-charges/000001a1-1094-d777-afb7-31fcc4b50000))

**FBI arrests a California real estate agent accused of spying on Taiwan's president's family for China.** Federal prosecutors say Wanying "Heather" Zhang, a 34-year-old Irvine realtor, was arrested at Los Angeles International Airport as she prepared to fly to Shanghai, accused of acting as an unregistered agent of the Chinese government. Prosecutors allege Zhang photographed and filmed the son of Taiwan President Lai Ching-te and his family outside their Seattle-area home in September 2025 and sent the images, video and license plate numbers to a Chinese official. *Why it matters:* It's the latest case of Beijing allegedly using ordinary-seeming intermediaries inside the US to track a foreign head of state's family on American soil. ([Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/oct/5/zhang-wanying-california-realtor-charged-spying-taiwan-president-lai/), [NewsNation](https://www.newsnationnow.com/crime/wanying-heather-zhang-spying-china-arrested/))

**Taiwan indicts four people over a Hong Kong-funded surveillance operation against a lawmaker.** Taipei prosecutors indicted four Taiwanese nationals, accusing them of being paid by two Hong Kong residents to plant a hidden camera outside the home of legislator Puma Shen, an advocate of strengthening Taiwan's defenses against China, to monitor him and his family. Prosecutors described the 2024 operation, funded by what they called an overseas hostile force, as a case of transnational repression. *Why it matters:* It shows Beijing-linked monitoring of a sitting Taiwanese lawmaker reaching into the physical space around his home, not just his communications. ([Hong Kong Free Press](https://hongkongfp.com/2026/10/07/taiwan-accuses-hong-kong-residents-of-hiring-4-people-to-spy-on-lawmaker-critical-of-china/))

**Three Iranians go on trial in London accused of spying on dissident journalists.** Mostafa Sepahvand, Farhad Javadi Manesh and Shapoor Qalehali Khani Noori are charged with engaging in conduct likely to assist Iran's foreign intelligence service by targeting journalists at Iran International, the London-based broadcaster frequently critical of Tehran, between August 2024 and February 2025. They are the first people charged under the UK's National Security Act of 2023, which covers threats from hostile states. *Why it matters:* It's a test of whether Britain's newest national security law can actually convict state-directed harassment of exiled journalists. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/three-iranians-trial-uk-spying-dissident-journalists-2026-10-06/))

**A former CIA officer pleads guilty after admitting he exposed a clandestine source to a foreign government.** David Rush pleaded guilty to wire fraud in a nearly $200 million scheme and separately admitted providing secrets, including the existence of a clandestine human source, to a foreign government; investigators found about 300 gold bars at his home. *Why it matters:* An intelligence officer burning a human source for personal enrichment rather than ideology is its own kind of counterintelligence failure. ([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cr3wvq965dngo))

**The UK's Security Service, MI5, tells universities to cut ties with a Chinese institute it calls an espionage front.** In its first-ever public "espionage alert," MI5 said the China General Technology Research Institute (CGTRI), part of a Chinese state-owned conglomerate, exists primarily to fund research that improves the Ministry of State Security's technical espionage capability, and that more than 100 UK-linked academics have contributed to CGTRI-funded projects on artificial intelligence, cybersecurity and covert communications. Security minister Dan Jarvis wrote to every university vice-chancellor urging them to end all arrangements with the institute. *Why it matters:* It's a rare case of a Western intelligence agency naming a specific foreign-funded research program as espionage rather than just warning about generic risk. ([International Business Times UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/mi5-warns-uk-universities-china-research-institute-1822941))

## US military and intelligence community

**Anduril and the Navy commit billions to a new submarine-component shipyard.** Anduril will invest $3.7 billion in a new "software-defined" shipyard in Baltimore County, Maryland, with the government taking an estimated 40% stake, as part of a $6.6 billion push with the Navy to boost production of components like torpedo tubes for Virginia-class attack submarines. *Why it matters:* A government equity stake in a contractor's shipyard is an unusual structure for trying to fix the Navy's chronic submarine production shortfall. ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/industry/techwatch/2026/10/06/anduril-us-navy-invest-66-billion-to-boost-submarine-parts-production/), [DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/10/06/white-house-anduril-announce-new-shipyard-baltimore-county/))

**The Army expands Anduril's role in its next-generation battle network with a $1.8 billion contract.** The Army awarded Anduril a contract worth up to $1.8 billion over five years to expand Next Generation Command and Control (NGC2), an ecosystem of connected hardware and software built on Anduril's Lattice software platform. *Why it matters:* It cements Anduril, rather than a traditional prime contractor, as a central vendor for how the Army links sensors and shooters. ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/10/06/army-awards-anduril-1-8b-contract-expand-ngc2/))

**Former NSA chief says the agency's reported overhaul is "probably needed."** Paul Nakasone said a reported reorganization of the National Security Agency is likely necessary to keep pace with faster-moving cyberthreats and AI competition, but warned its success depends on execution and a workforce facing mounting retirement pressure. *Why it matters:* A former director publicly endorsing a shakeup of his old agency, with a caveat about workforce strain, is a notable signal about how seriously NSA's current leadership views the problem. ([CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/nsa-reorganization-paul-nakasone-ai-cyberthreats/))

**House Democrats press the FBI over a planned nationwide license-plate camera network.** Representative Greg Casar, the top Democrat on a House Oversight subcommittee overseeing federal law enforcement, pushed the FBI for details on its plan to spend $36 million for direct access to a nationwide automatic license plate reader network capable of tracking vehicles in near real time, warning it risks "substantial abuses of Americans' data." Flock Safety and Motorola Solutions are among the likely bidders. *Why it matters:* Congress is now treating the FBI's own surveillance buildout, not just private vendors like Flock, as the oversight problem. ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/10/02/2026/house-democrat-presses-fbi-on-license-plate-readers))

## China, Russia, Iran and North Korea

**EU ambassadors agree on the bloc's largest-ever Russia sanctions package.** The package adds roughly 1,576 individuals and entities, mostly tied to Russia's defense-industrial complex, including shipyards, electronics makers and armored-vehicle repair facilities, and separately sanctions 77 candidates who stood in Russia's State Duma elections held in occupied Ukrainian territory. EU foreign ministers are expected to formally adopt it October 12 in Luxembourg. *Why it matters:* The scale, the largest single addition since the sanctions regime began in 2014, signals the EU still sees economic pressure as its main lever even as the war grinds on. ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/exclusive-eu-readies-biggest-ever-sanctions-package-targeting-russias-war-machine/))

**China opens a new military base in Laos, its second in Southeast Asia.** Beijing's new facility in Laos is its third overseas military base and its second in the region, following its base in Cambodia. *Why it matters:* A second Southeast Asian base extends China's basing footprint along routes that matter for a Taiwan contingency and for the South China Sea. ([Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2026/10/07/china-military-base-laos-vientiane/))

**Vance says Iran must cut nuclear enrichment to end the war with the US.** Vice President JD Vance said Washington is in talks with Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, and that ending the war requires Iran to curtail its uranium enrichment program. *Why it matters:* It's the clearest public statement yet of what the administration considers the minimum condition for ending the conflict. ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/us-news/2026-10-07/ty-article/vance-iran-must-cut-nuclear-enrichment-to-end-war-with-u-s/000001a1-1454-df57-a7e5-3cd41d3d0000))

## Germany and Israel

**Shin Bet, police and IDF chiefs coordinate security for Israel's upcoming election.** All three of Israel's security authority chiefs said they met to discuss measures to "ensure the proper and secure conduct" of the national election scheduled for October 27. *Why it matters:* Top-level coordination this far ahead signals how seriously Israel's security establishment is treating the risk of unrest or interference around the vote. ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/elections/2026-10-06/ty-article/.premium/shin-bet-police-idf-chiefs-discuss-safe-conduct-of-upcoming-israeli-election/000001a1-11bf-dd49-a5ff-d3bfdefe0000))

**The Bundeswehr gets exclusive access to radar satellites for NATO's eastern flank.** Germany's armed forces, the Bundeswehr, can for the first time use radar satellites from Finnish company ICEYE for operations in Lithuania and surveillance of NATO's eastern flank, under a joint venture between ICEYE and German defense firm Rheinmetall. *Why it matters:* It gives Germany independent overhead surveillance of Russia's border region rather than relying solely on allied or commercial imagery. ([Augen geradeaus!, in German](https://augengeradeaus.net/2026/10/finnische-radarsatelliten-fuer-bundeswehr-ueberwachung-der-nato-ostflanke-einsatzbereit/))

**An Israel Defense Forces (IDF) intelligence officer contests his removal over October 7 failures.** Lt. Col. Aryeh Amidror, the intelligence officer for the IDF's Gaza Division on October 7, is challenging his ouster, saying he was not responsible for the lack of warning before the Hamas attack and that he is "the only commander paying a real price." *Why it matters:* Disputes like this one keep the question of individual versus institutional accountability for October 7 open three years on. ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/israel-security/2026-10-06/ty-article/.premium/israeli-gaza-border-intelligence-officer-i-didnt-fail-in-my-role-on-oct-7/000001a1-10a7-dee5-a5b9-b2ff0d8f0000))
