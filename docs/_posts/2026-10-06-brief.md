**Bottom line:** German federal prosecutors arrested former BND president August Hanning on charges of treasonous espionage, accusing him of selling roughly 2,000 classified intelligence files to a foreign spy service over more than a decade. The Justice Department separately moved to strip citizenship from 40 people, including a Navy sailor convicted of spying for China, and Iran nominated a sanctions-listed missile official as its new defense minister. In Berlin, intelligence chiefs again warned parliament that Russia is waging a "shadow war" against Germany, while Israel's Shin Bet joined the interrogation of the flydubai hijacking suspect in the UAE.

## Espionage and counterintelligence

**Germany arrests its own former spy chief on treason charges.** Federal prosecutors arrested August Hanning, who led the Bundesnachrichtendienst (BND), Germany's foreign intelligence service, from 1998 to 2005, along with his former chief of staff, identified only as Manfred D. Prosecutors allege that starting around 2010, Manfred D. supplied Hanning with nearly 2,000 classified BND documents in exchange for payment, which Hanning used in his private consulting work and to brief a contact in a foreign intelligence service on German political and security matters over several years. *Why it matters:* A former head of Germany's foreign intelligence service allegedly selling the agency's own secrets for more than a decade, if proven, would be one of the most serious breaches in the BND's history. ([Generalbundesanwalt, in German](https://www.presseportal.de/blaulicht/pm/14981/6365655), [ZDFheute, in German](https://www.zdfheute.de/politik/deutschland/august-hanning-ex-bnd-chef-spionage-100.html))

**The Justice Department moves to strip citizenship from 40 people, including a Navy sailor who spied for China.** The department filed denaturalization complaints in federal courts against 40 individuals in what it called its largest volume of such filings in a single period, among them Jinchao Wei, a former Navy sailor convicted of selling US Navy secrets to a Chinese intelligence officer for $12,000 while concealing the scheme to obtain citizenship through expedited military-service naturalization. *Why it matters:* The administration is treating citizenship obtained through concealed espionage convictions as reversible, not just prosecutable. ([Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-moves-strip-citizenship-40-criminals-largest-single-period))

**New reporting says Canadian officials spied for the US in Cuba around the time of the alleged "sonic attacks."** The Globe and Mail reported that a Canadian diplomat gathered intelligence and shared it directly with American intelligence officers in Havana between 2016 and 2018, and that Canadians used satellite equipment brought into Cuba by the United States, around the same period diplomats from both countries began reporting the neurological symptoms later dubbed Havana syndrome. *Why it matters:* It adds a previously unreported intelligence-sharing angle to one of the central unsolved mysteries in recent US-Canada security history. ([The Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/politics/article-canadians-spying-for-us-in-cuba-at-time-alleged-sonic-attacks-source/))

## US military and intelligence community

**Trump puts his Director of National Intelligence in charge of White House AI policy.** Jay Clayton, the Director of National Intelligence (DNI), will now also lead broad domestic technology and economic policy discussions on artificial intelligence for the White House. *Why it matters:* Putting the nation's top intelligence official in charge of AI policy signals the administration is approaching the technology primarily as a security issue. ([Defense One](https://www.defenseone.com/policy/2026/10/us-intel-chief-white-house-ai-czar/416416/))

**A Pentagon and FBI personnel-data breach exposed about 3 million people and went undetected for months.** Defense One reported the breach affected personnel records across both agencies and was not discovered until well after it occurred. *Why it matters:* A breach of that scale going undetected for months raises questions about monitoring of systems holding sensitive personnel data across the defense and law enforcement workforce. ([Defense One](https://www.defenseone.com/threats/2026/10/pentagon-personnel-data-breach-went-undetected-months/416421/))

**The Pentagon stands up a four-star command for autonomous warfare.** The Secretary of War announced the new Autonomous Warfare Command at Quantico on September 30, giving officials 30 days to produce an implementation plan and setting October 1, 2027, as the date the command becomes operational. *Why it matters:* Creating a dedicated four-star command is a structural bet that autonomous systems, not just individual drone programs, now warrant their own chain of command. ([The Cipher Brief](https://www.thecipherbrief.com/the-pentagons-2027-drone-problem))

## China, Russia, Iran and North Korea

**Iran nominates a sanctions-listed missile official as its new defense minister.** President Masoud Pezeshkian nominated Mehrdad Akhlaghi Ketabchi, a longtime defense ministry official with deep ties to Iran's missile and aerospace sectors who has been under UN sanctions since 2007 for heading an entity linked to Iran's ballistic missile program, to fill the post left vacant since Defense Minister Aziz Nasirzadeh was killed in US-Israeli strikes in February. The nomination goes to parliament for a confidence vote. *Why it matters:* Tapping a sanctioned missile-industry veteran to run the defense ministry signals Tehran's near-term priority is rebuilding its missile arsenal, not courting sanctions relief. ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/mena/pezeshkian-nominates-sanctions-listed-mehrdad-akhlaghi-ketabchi-as-iran-defence-minister-1.500699695))

**US officials reportedly feared an Iran-linked drone strike on a UK base from within British soil.** The New York Times reported that American officials feared a multipronged drone attack on RAF Fairford, which hosts US bombers, loosely modeled on Ukraine's deep strikes inside Russia. *Why it matters:* The reported plot would have adapted a Ukrainian tactic to threaten US airpower on allied territory, inside NATO's own borders. ([Haaretz, citing The New York Times](https://www.haaretz.com/middle-east-news/iran/2026-10-06/ty-article/report-iran-suspected-of-drone-plot-against-u-k-base-hosting-u-s-bombers/000001a1-0fb4-d777-afb7-3ffc5b5b0000))

**Ukraine says Russia is using chemical agents as a routine battlefield tactic.** Ukraine's General Staff has recorded nearly 14,000 cases of Russian forces using hazardous chemical substances on the front line as of September, mainly delivered through hand-thrown gas grenades and improvised devices. *Why it matters:* A nearly 14,000-incident count, if accurate, points to chemical weapons use as standard Russian practice rather than isolated incidents. ([Jamestown Foundation](https://jamestown.org/russian-military-uses-chemical-agents-as-a-standard-battlefield-tactic/))

**A researcher at a Russian plague laboratory died of a suspected infection, and Trump says the US is watching closely.** A 28-year-old worker at an anti-plague research institute in Siberia died of what Russian authorities described as an infection of unknown origin, possibly plague, with nearly 200 contacts placed under medical observation; Trump said the US was looking "very strongly" at the case. *Why it matters:* A death at a Russian biological research facility, even one Russian authorities call accidental, draws scrutiny given the facility's work with dangerous pathogens. ([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cv5yn3nwp5n5o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss))

**The US Marine Corps will deploy anti-ship and air-defense systems to a remote Japanese island near Taiwan.** The exercise this month will put Marine ground-based anti-ship and air-defense systems on Japan's southwesternmost island. *Why it matters:* Positioning these systems so close to Taiwan signals the US is rehearsing the kind of island-chain defenses it would rely on in a Taiwan contingency. ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/global/asia-pacific/2026/10/05/us-marine-corps-anti-ship-weaponry-to-see-exercise-action-on-remote-japan-island-near-taiwan/))

## Germany and Israel

**Germany's BND chief tells parliament Russia is waging a "shadow war" against the country.** At the Bundestag's Parliamentary Control Panel hearing, held publicly once a year, BND President Martin Jäger said Germany "is in danger of sliding into a violent conflict with Russia" and that Russian intelligence services act "with high professionalism and ruthlessness," pointing to the failed Leipzig airport drone plot as one example. *Why it matters:* It's the second year running that Germany's own intelligence chief has used the hearing to publicly frame Russian activity as an active, not hypothetical, conflict. ([Tagesspiegel, in German](https://www.tagesspiegel.de/politik/dpa-russland-fuhrt-einen-schattenkrieg-gegen-uns-bnd-chef-warnt-vor-gewaltsamem-konflikt-zwischen-deutschland-und-russland-16125192.html))

**Germany's domestic intelligence agency warns Iran is stepping up efforts to acquire German missile technology.** The Bundesamt für Verfassungsschutz (BfV) reported "consistently high" and rising Iranian procurement activity targeting German missile and rocket technology, saying Iran is trying to rebuild weapons stocks and production facilities destroyed in last year's war, while sanctions have mainly pushed Iranian procurement networks toward "concealing and conspiratorial behavior" rather than stopping it. *Why it matters:* The warning suggests sanctions are shaping how Iran procures weapons technology in Germany, not whether it keeps trying. ([Berlin Story News, in German](https://www.berlinstory.de/news/verfassungsschutz-warnt-vor-iranischen-beschaffungsaktivitaeten))

**Haaretz reports Shin Bet refrained from investigating a professor accused of plotting mass attacks on Arab civilians.** The lecturer had a licensed weapon and a notebook detailing attack plans and recruitment of activists, but senior Shin Bet officials ordered the agency not to investigate despite the case falling under its purview, and police were slow to act. *Why it matters:* A domestic security service allegedly declining to act on a documented mass-casualty plot is a serious oversight failure, whatever the eventual legal outcome. ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/israel-security/2026-10-05/ty-article/.premium/shin-bet-refrained-from-probing-professor-who-planned-attacks-on-arab-civilians/000001a1-0cef-d344-adbd-cfff40550000))

**Shin Bet agents joined the UAE interrogation of the flydubai hijacking suspect.** Israeli officials said copilot Hamam al-Hammami, who allegedly tried to crash a Dubai-to-Tel Aviv flight into Ben Gurion Airport, told interrogators he acted out of "revenge for Gaza"; Israeli officials said their current assessment is that he acted alone, though they are still examining a possible Iran connection. *Why it matters:* Israel's domestic security service taking an active investigative role on foreign soil shows how seriously it is treating an attack aimed at its own airport. ([The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/shin-bet-agents-participating-in-interrogation-of-flydubai-terrorist-in-uae-toi-learns/))
