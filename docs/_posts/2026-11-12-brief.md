## Espionage and counterintelligence

**Russian hackers breached Romanian government servers to harvest NATO and EU emails.** A Slovak cybersecurity firm found that hackers linked to Russia's FSB quietly compromised a Romanian government organization's servers in December 2025 to collect emails related to NATO and the EU. *Why it matters:* a quiet, monthslong intrusion into a NATO and EU member's government systems shows Russian intelligence still has effective access to allied networks even as attention stays fixed on the war in Ukraine. ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/11/11/romanian-servers-targeted-by-russian-hackers-to-scoop-up-emails-about-nato-eu/))

**Britain and France issue first guidelines for commercial hacking firms.** The two governments called on businesses that develop, sell or finance commercial hacking capabilities to vet their customers, build in technical safeguards against misuse, and maintain systems for monitoring and reporting abuse. *Why it matters:* it's an early attempt by two major democracies to regulate the commercial spyware industry that has let governments and criminals alike buy hacking tools once limited to top intelligence services. ([The Record](https://therecord.media/britain-france-set-out-guidelines-for-commercial-hacking-firms))

## US military and intelligence community

**CRS flags strategic stability risks in Golden Dome report to Congress.** The Congressional Research Service delivered Congress a report examining the second Trump administration's proposed "next-generation missile defense shield" against nuclear and conventional threats, laying out strategic stability considerations lawmakers should weigh as the program advances. *Why it matters:* a homeland missile shield ambitious enough to blunt an adversary's nuclear deterrent can itself unsettle the balance of power, and Congress is being told to think that through before funding it further. ([USNI News](https://news.usni.org/2026/11/11/report-to-congress-on-strategic-stability-of-the-golden-dome))

**Poland says the Pentagon backs a permanent US base, decision due in December.** Warsaw has identified four potential sites for a base that could house 5,000 US troops and their families, and has offered to spend up to $4.4 billion on supporting infrastructure. *Why it matters:* a permanent rather than rotational US presence in Poland would be one of the most concrete signs yet that Washington plans to keep reinforcing NATO's eastern flank for the long term. ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/poland-says-pentagon-supports-permanent-us-base-with-decision-expected-in-december/))

**Army works to unify command and control across thousands of miles.** The Army's Next Generation Command and Control (NGC2) effort aims to connect data across units, services and allies so commanders can act faster, a challenge made harder in the Pacific where adversaries jam communications and track emissions across vast distances. *Why it matters:* breaking down stovepiped systems is meant to let layered air and missile defenses cue each other fast enough to matter, and a modular design would let the Army swap out vulnerable hardware without rebuilding the whole network. ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/the-armys-coming-c2-challenge-is-fighting-across-thousands-of-miles/))

## China, Russia, Iran and North Korea

**Kushner and Witkoff head to Moscow then Dublin after inconclusive Abu Dhabi talks.** The Abu Dhabi round, the first trilateral US-Ukraine-Russia talks since February, produced only an in-principle prisoner swap and no movement on a ceasefire or territory, even as Washington broadened the agenda to include a European security architecture and reportedly floated NATO Article 5-style guarantees for Ukraine; US envoys will now seek Russia's buy-in on European participation in Moscow before meeting Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky in Dublin. *Why it matters:* expanding the talks to cover Europe's broader security order, not just Ukraine, raises the stakes of a negotiation one expert says shows no clear step toward peace yet. ([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cwrkgmymk55zo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss), [Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/content-series/fastthinking/where-the-russia-ukraine-peace-talks-stand-after-abu-dhabi/))

**Ukraine says Russia used a humanitarian pause to reinforce Oleshky.** A ceasefire brokered by Melania Trump let aid reach the Russian-held Dnipro River town of Oleshky and let residents evacuate, but Ukrainian officials say Russia used the pause to move in more than 1,000 troops along with drones and artillery and rebuild its positions; Russia has not commented and the claims are unverified. *Why it matters:* if humanitarian pauses let an occupying force resupply rather than just protect civilians, they get harder for Ukraine to agree to even when the need is real. ([The War Zone](https://www.twz.com/news-features/ukraine-regretting-white-house-brokered-temporary-ceasefire-in-strategic-riverside-town-of-oleshky))

**Iran's security chief threatens to strike US bases in Europe.** Mohsen Rezaei said that if the United States attacks Iran, it will hit places it has not targeted before, and separately said Iran needs enriched uranium to power nuclear submarines and ships. *Why it matters:* threatening US bases on NATO soil, not just in the Gulf, would widen any US-Iran conflict well beyond the Middle East. ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/world-news/europe/2026-11-11/ty-article/iran-says-it-will-target-u-s-bases-in-europe-if-american-jets-use-them-in-war/000001a1-cb3b-dfb8-adef-ff7fad3b0000))

**NATO uses Ukrainian tactics to pressure-test its own forces.** NATO has started using Ukrainian troops and Ukrainian-made uncrewed surface vessels as a "red team" in exercises meant to probe gaps in alliance forces, according to Admiral Pierre Vandier. *Why it matters:* building battle-tested Ukrainian tactics directly into NATO's own training signals the alliance expects to keep adapting to Russia's playbook well past any ceasefire. ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/nato-taps-ukraine-as-red-team-in-drills-to-trigger-an-adaptation-cycle/))

## Germany and Israel

**Shin Bet chief doubles down on election-interference probe despite internal pushback.** Haaretz reports that Shin Bet chief David Zini approved far-reaching measures in a foreign election-interference investigation, pressing ahead days after Israeli police said the agency's evidence in a related case was insufficient; a senior defense official told Haaretz that resources are being diverted from national-security missions, and another official said people in the agency "are going very far to please the Shin Bet chief." *Why it matters:* allegations that a domestic security service is overreaching on a politically sensitive probe raise questions about how closely its leadership is being held to account. ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/israel-politics/2026-11-11/ty-article/.premium/official-shin-bet-head-oks-special-measures-in-foreign-election-influence-probe/000001a1-cab4-d4a1-a9a9-fff7748b0000))

**Former military chief accuses Netanyahu of using Iran war as a political lifeline.** Haaretz reports that Netanyahu appears to be reigniting tensions with Iran to help him stay in power under cover of war, a view backed by a public warning from Gadi Eisenkot, a former Israel Defense Forces (IDF) chief of staff, who echoed the sitting IDF chief's concern that renewed fighting could derail Israel's political transition. *Why it matters:* a former top general publicly challenging the sitting prime minister's motives for risking a wider war is a serious rift within Israel's security establishment. ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/israel-security/2026-11-11/ty-article/.premium/netanyahu-is-desperate-to-stay-in-power-and-eyes-iran-war-as-potential-lifeline/000001a1-cbc2-d773-a5bd-efdf63830000))

**Israeli officials warn the country isn't ready for a Ukraine-style drone attack from within.** A year after Ukraine's drone strikes stunned Russia, Netanyahu has warned a similar operation could be launched from within Israel, and security officials say the country isn't prepared for it. *Why it matters:* officials voicing doubt about Israel's defenses against a domestic drone-smuggling attack flag a capability gap that Iran has reason to test. ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/security-aviation/2026-11-11/ty-article-magazine/drones-from-within-could-iran-launch-its-own-operation-spiderweb-in-israel/000001a1-bffe-d7bb-a7a5-ffff7e900000))
