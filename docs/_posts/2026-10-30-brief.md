**Bottom line:** Israeli military prosecutors indicted a soldier for passing Iron Dome and troop-readiness details to an Iranian handler for cryptocurrency, while Romanian intelligence says it broke up a Russian-coordinated sabotage network behind a fire at an arms plant, with suspects held in Germany and Poland. The US is weighing new military options against Iran as transport aircraft land in Israel, and Israel's Shin Bet is pressing its probe into alleged Qatari and Turkish interference in last week's election.

## Espionage and counterintelligence

**An Israeli soldier is charged with feeding Iran a year of military secrets for cryptocurrency.** Military prosecutors indicted the soldier on Thursday for passing information to the enemy and contact with a foreign agent. He is accused of maintaining contact with an Iranian handler for about a year, reporting his unit's activities, IDF readiness and call-ups, sending base locations and coordinates, and photographing interceptors and US refueling aircraft at an Israeli airport, in exchange for cryptocurrency payments; prosecutors say he was also offered $200,000 to travel to Iran. *Why it matters:* It's the latest in a string of Iranian cryptocurrency-for-access recruitment cases among Israeli soldiers since the war with Iran began. ([Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/idf-soldier-charged-over-spying-for-iran-in-exchange-for-cryptocurrency/))

**Romania says it broke up a Russian-coordinated sabotage network behind a fire at an arms plant, with suspects held in Germany and Poland.** Romania's intelligence service said a Russia-coordinated network was behind a September fire near the Carfil arms factory in Brașov, which supplies Romania's state defense group. Two Belarusian suspects were detained in Germany and a Ukrainian suspect in Poland; Bucharest issued European arrest warrants and summoned Russia's ambassador to protest. Moscow has denied organizing sabotage in Europe. *Why it matters:* It's part of a wider pattern of suspected Russian sabotage plots against defense-industrial sites across NATO's eastern members. ([Romania Insider](https://www.romania-insider.com/romanian-intelligence-links-fire-carfil-industrial-platform-russian))

**Poland detained a Russian man after he was caught photographing rail infrastructure.** Police in Świdnica said a 55-year-old Russian national was found with thousands of photos of key sites and transport facilities, including locations outside Poland, which investigators believe were meant for a foreign intelligence service. A court ordered him held for three months on a charge of acting on behalf of foreign intelligence against Poland. *Why it matters:* It's the latest in a steady run of Polish arrests this year tied to suspected Russian reconnaissance and sabotage planning. ([LIGA.net](https://news.liga.net/en/world/news/a-russian-national-was-detained-in-poland-on-suspicion-of-espionage))

**A Syrian man was sentenced to 42 months for hacking on behalf of the Assad regime.** A federal court in the Eastern District of Virginia sentenced Ahmad Umar Agha for his role in a hacking conspiracy organized by members of former Syrian President Bashar al-Assad's inner circle, including a commander of Syria's security forces, the Justice Department said. *Why it matters:* It shows US prosecutors are still working through hacking cases tied to the former Syrian regime even after its fall. ([Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/syrian-man-sentenced-wide-ranging-hacking-conspiracy-conducted-behalf-bashar-al-assad-regime))

**A leaked archive of Department of Homeland Security employee data is proving hard to take down.** CyberScoop reported that the "Icepick" database of DHS personnel information has faced repeated takedown attempts, including a removal request from a DHS contractor and a block by a social media platform, but kept finding a new home online. *Why it matters:* Once personnel data like this leaks, takedown requests can't fully undo the exposure for the employees affected. ([CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/dhs-employee-breach-icepick-database-takedown/))

## US military and intelligence community

**The Pentagon fired a senior general over an alleged classified leak.** Defense News reported that the Pentagon fired US Army Lt. Gen. Brett Sylvia, a senior military planner on the Joint Staff, over accusations he leaked classified information to the public. *Why it matters:* A Joint Staff planner's firing over a leak points to internal friction over how the administration is handling sensitive information, including on Iran. ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/pentagon/2026/10/29/senior-us-general-fired-accused-of-leaking-classified-information/))

**Two FBI agents charged with lying to Congress had a preliminary hearing this week.** Lawfare live-blogged the hearing before a magistrate judge in the case against the two agents. *Why it matters:* Charges against FBI agents for statements to Congress raise the stakes for how the bureau's own personnel account for their conduct under oversight. ([Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/fbi-agents-charged-over-lying-to-congress--a-hearing-diary))

**A top Army Pacific commander said the "2027 window" for a possible Chinese move on Taiwan still holds.** Gen. Ronald Clark said Army Pacific is positioning personnel and equipment across the region to meet that benchmark and deter a potential Chinese attack on Taiwan, Breaking Defense reported. *Why it matters:* The US is still planning and resourcing around a near-term Taiwan contingency, not treating the risk as having passed. ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/taiwan-2027-conflict-window-remains-valid-army-pacific-commander-says/))

## China, Russia, Iran and North Korea

**The US is weighing new military options against Iran as transport aircraft land in Israel.** US officials briefed Trump on military options against Iran this week, and roughly 10 US military transport planes landed at Israeli bases, according to reporting followed live by Fox News. Separately, Haaretz reported that Israeli defense officials doubt a short US-led air campaign could topple the government in Tehran or halt its nuclear program, and warn that Prime Minister Netanyahu's objectives for another round of fighting differ from Trump's. *Why it matters:* Washington and Jerusalem appear to want different outcomes from a renewed campaign, which could complicate any decision to strike. ([Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/live-news/us-iran-war-trump-military-options-strait-hormuz-10-29-26), [Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/middle-east-news/2026-10-30/ty-article/.premium/idf-fears-u-s-may-seek-short-iran-war-that-ends-before-israel-meets-goals/000001a1-8bc6-d666-afed-dbe736860000))

**North Korea is blamed for a record $6.3 billion crypto heist at Binance.** The Record reported that Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, confirmed it was hacked for $6.3 billion, with blockchain investigators pointing to North Korea. *Why it matters:* It would be North Korea's largest crypto theft to date, funding a program that has long relied on hacking to evade sanctions. ([The Record](https://therecord.media/binance-hit-by-record-6-billion-crypto-hack))

**Russia is leaning on stealth and coercion to keep its mobilization going.** Foreign Policy reported that with voluntary military recruitment falling short, the Kremlin has shifted to quieter forms of pressure and coercion to keep pulling men into the war. *Why it matters:* Reliance on coercion over volunteers suggests Russia's manpower pipeline for Ukraine is under more strain than the Kremlin lets on. ([Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2026/10/30/putin-russia-ukraine-war-mobilization-casualties/))

**US intelligence released its clearest photo yet of China's secretive Shenlong spaceplane.** A Defense Intelligence Agency report included a satellite image showing the reusable spaceplane's underside and solar arrays, taken during an earlier orbital mission; the program has drawn attention for releasing and later recapturing objects in orbit. *Why it matters:* Shenlong's continued secrecy, paired with its object-release maneuvers, feeds US concern about China's progress on space-based military capabilities. ([Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/chinas-secretive-space-plane-caught-on-camera-in-incredible-new-photo-released-by-us-military))

## Germany and Israel

**Israel's Shin Bet is pressing ahead with its probe into alleged Qatari and Turkish election interference.** Haaretz reported that the domestic security agency's inquiry into claims of foreign interference tied to Arab voter turnout does not implicate the whole group of voters, even as Prime Minister Netanyahu and allied politicians have alleged that Qatari funding drove the turnout. A separate Haaretz piece traces the probe back to a Likud-driven campaign alleging fraud among Arab voters. *Why it matters:* An intelligence probe into an election outcome, launched amid claims from the governing coalition itself, risks becoming a tool in a political fight rather than a neutral inquiry. ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/elections/2026-10-29/ty-article/.premium/shin-bet-election-interference-probe-does-not-implicate-whole-group-of-voters/000001a1-8858-d125-a3b1-edde70160000), [Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/elections/2026-10-29/ty-article/.premium/did-likuds-scare-campaign-against-arab-voters-lead-to-a-shin-bet-probe/000001a1-87bf-dabc-afb5-ffff151f0000))

**Germany's new brigade in Lithuania will use electronic warfare to watch Kaliningrad and Belarus, as reports say the Bundeswehr still trails Russia in the field.** Reuters reported that Germany's permanent brigade in Lithuania will use electronic-warfare capabilities to provide early warning of military activity in Kaliningrad and Belarus, according to the military's cyber chief. Germany's own officers have said Russia fields five electronic-warfare brigades to Germany's more limited capacity and uses the tools offensively in Ukraine, manipulating navigation and communications. *Why it matters:* Germany is building a frontline sensor capability before it has closed the underlying gap with Russian electronic warfare. ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/10/29/germany-brigade-in-lithuania-to-monitor-kaliningrad-belarus-with-electronic-warfare))

**German intelligence agencies reportedly plan a shared classified-data platform built on Google infrastructure.** According to NDR and WDR reporting cited by German outlets, the BND, the domestic intelligence agency BfV and the military counterintelligence service MAD want a joint platform, reportedly worth around 500 million euros, built on an air-gapped version of Google's cloud to exchange secret-level data; the agencies declined to comment. Critics call reliance on a US tech giant for secret-level data a sovereignty problem. *Why it matters:* A foreign vendor underpinning Germany's most sensitive intelligence-sharing infrastructure raises hard questions about technical sovereignty, even with an air-gapped setup. ([Mobiflip, in German](https://www.mobiflip.de/?p=493548))
