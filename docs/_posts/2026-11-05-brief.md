## Espionage and counterintelligence

**Two Chinese nationals were sentenced to 30 months in prison each for acting as unregistered agents of China's Ministry of State Security.** Liren "Ryan" Lai, 40, and Yuance Chen, 39, pleaded guilty to collecting roughly $30,000 in cash drops, including $10,000 left in a locker at a Livermore, California recreation center and $20,000 in Phoenix, while working to recruit US Navy personnel to spy for Beijing. US District Judge Jon Tigar also imposed three years of supervised release; both men were arrested in June 2025. *Why it matters:* The case shows Chinese intelligence still relies on old-fashioned cash dead drops alongside cyber tools to build networks inside the US military. ([Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/two-chinese-nationals-sentenced-to-prison-for-spying-on-us-navy/), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/31/us-jails-two-chinese-nationals-for-spying))

**A US blockchain intelligence firm said a North Korean unit was likely behind a hack that hit crypto exchange Binance for more than $6 billion.** The firm's initial investigation pointed to a compromised laptop, but a follow-up review turned up additional "points of breach," according to reporting relayed by the Associated Press. *Why it matters:* The scale of the alleged theft shows North Korea's state-backed hacking units now operate at a level that can move global crypto markets, not just fund the regime. ([WBTV/AP](https://www.wbtv.com/2026/10/30/north-korea-linked-63-billion-hack-binance-blockchain-intelligence-firms-say/))

**A Canadian national was extradited to the United States to face charges over a hacking and illicit cryptomining scheme that hit school districts in at least four states.** The Justice Department said the case, which dates back to 2016, involved two identified suspects who allegedly ran cryptomining operations on compromised computers belonging to US educational institutions. *Why it matters:* The case is a reminder that financially motivated hacking of under-resourced public institutions like school districts can run undetected for years. ([The Record](https://therecord.media/canadian-nationals-accused-us-education-hacks-cryptomining))

**French bank Crédit Mutuel Arkéa confirmed hackers stole an estimated $133 million after compromising an unnamed supplier.** The bank said in a notice that the attackers used the access to make transfers out of the bank's own account. *Why it matters:* The breach shows attackers are increasingly reaching banks through trusted third-party suppliers rather than attacking defenses head on. ([The Record](https://therecord.media/french-bank-confirms-cyber-heist))

## US military and intelligence community

**The Pentagon's push to field artificial intelligence tools is outrunning its ability to test them, The Cipher Brief reported, citing a recent case where an AI-assisted report nearly caused a dangerous mistake.** CNN reported, citing four sources, that the US military prepared to intercept a Chinese vessel after an AI-assisted intelligence report incorrectly concluded the ship was carrying nuclear weapons components. *Why it matters:* An AI misread that nearly triggered a military intercept of a foreign vessel shows the risk of fielding AI tools faster than the Pentagon can verify their judgment. ([The Cipher Brief](https://www.thecipherbrief.com/in-its-race-to-field-ai-the-pentagons-testing-cant-keep-up))

**Space Force leaders say they are drawing lessons from a recent close pass by Russian satellites near a US commercial satellite.** Gen. Douglas Schiess, the Space Force's top operational commander, said intelligence officials are "making sure we get lessons learned" from the incident, which he said reinforced the need for the service to be able to defend US space assets. *Why it matters:* Russian satellites shadowing US commercial space assets raise the stakes for a domain where Washington has few established rules of the road. ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/04/space-force-gen-schiess-russian-satellite-maneuvers/))

**The House China committee is pressing the Air Force not to cut funding for its main in-house think tank on Chinese military power.** In a letter to Air Force Secretary Troy Meink, the committee warned that "under-resourcing" the China Aerospace Studies Institute "could risk undermining the ability of military leaders and civilian policymakers to understand" Beijing's aerospace strategy. *Why it matters:* Cutting the Pentagon's own China-watching capacity would be a strange place to economize while warning of a rising Chinese military threat. ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/house-china-committee-pushes-air-force-on-think-tank-cuts/))

## China, Russia, Iran and North Korea

**Russia's senior leadership has issued a string of explicit warnings this fall that it will defend Kaliningrad by "all available means," implicitly including nuclear weapons, Jamestown Foundation reported.** The warnings began in early September with remarks from a deputy foreign minister and have continued as Moscow watches NATO's posture near the exclave. *Why it matters:* Explicit nuclear-adjacent rhetoric over Kaliningrad raises the risk of miscalculation in an already tense Baltic theater. ([Jamestown Foundation](https://jamestown.org/moscow-expresses-warnings-on-kaliningrad-defense-against-nato/))

**Ukraine says a Russian "double-tap" strike killed at least 40 people in a village, with another 30 feared still under rubble.** Kyiv said the attack, which happened Monday, was captured on bodycam video. *Why it matters:* Double-tap strikes, which hit the same target twice to catch rescuers, are a tactic Ukraine has repeatedly accused Russia of using against civilians. ([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c98rz6penv86o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss))

**Pakistan briefly barred Taiwanese airlines from flying over its territory before abruptly reversing the decision, The Diplomat reported.** The outlet raised the possibility that the reversal reflected pressure tied to Beijing's broader strategy toward Taiwan, though it did not confirm Chinese involvement. *Why it matters:* Even an aborted overflight ban shows how China's neighbors and partners can be used as pressure points against Taiwan without Beijing acting directly. ([The Diplomat](https://thediplomat.com/2026/11/pakistans-abrupt-and-aborted-revocation-of-taiwans-overflight-privileges/))

**China is pushing the yuan into more of Russia's and Iran's trade without actually displacing the dollar's global role, The Diplomat argued.** The analysis says both sanctioned countries can settle transactions in yuan, but the proceeds mostly stay trapped inside China's financial system by design. *Why it matters:* Beijing is positioning itself as the financial backstop for sanctioned states without taking on the risks of a true dollar rival. ([The Diplomat](https://thediplomat.com/2026/11/china-is-defanging-the-dollar-not-dethroning-it/))

## Germany and Israel

**Israel's Shin Bet chief is pushing to widen his investigation into alleged foreign election interference over the attorney general's objections.** David Zini wants to investigate funds he alleges were transferred from Arab states to influence the vote, but the attorney general is urging caution. Zini says the relevant intelligence on the alleged transfers mostly arrived on Election Day itself, which he says is why they could not be stopped in time. *Why it matters:* An open rift between Israel's domestic security chief and its top legal authority over an unproven election-interference claim threatens to undermine confidence in the vote count itself. ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/elections/2026-11-05/ty-article/.premium/shin-bet-chief-pushes-to-expand-election-probe-amid-clash-with-attorney-general/000001a1-a839-db68-a5bb-a9fb554a0000))

**A journalist related to an Arab party leader was arrested in an Israeli police raid tied to the same probe.** Sami Abu Shehadeh accused Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Shin Bet chief Zini of trying to "steal the elections," calling the arrest a serious crime. *Why it matters:* The arrest of a journalist connected to the investigation's target feeds opposition charges that the probe is being used to intimidate critics rather than pursue evidence. ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/israel-security/2026-11-04/ty-article/.premium/journalist-related-to-arab-party-leader-arrested-in-israel-police-raid/000001a1-a5db-d1fc-aba1-e5fb29190000))

**Mossad chief Roman Gofman is continuing to purge the agency's senior ranks, Haaretz reported.** The head of Mossad's combat unit is set to be replaced by an official described as a close associate of Gofman, who has already dismissed roughly a quarter of the agency's senior management since taking over. *Why it matters:* A leadership purge of this scale at Israel's main foreign intelligence service risks disrupting institutional knowledge during an active regional conflict. ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/israel-security/2026-11-04/ty-article/.premium/a-real-shake-up-top-mossad-official-fired-over-disagreements-with-new-chief/000001a1-a7dc-d436-a5a3-ffde22840000))

**An internal Israeli investigation found that Shin Bet and the Israel Defense Forces knew Hamas military chief Mohammed Deif had gone underground hours before the October 7 attack.** The inquiry found failures in intelligence analysis and decision-making but did not attribute negligence to any specific party; the military maintains the full picture could not have been understood that night. *Why it matters:* The finding adds to a growing body of evidence that Israeli intelligence had scattered warning signs before October 7 that were not pieced together in time. ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/israel-security/2026-11-04/ty-article/.premium/shin-bet-and-idf-knew-hamas-military-chief-went-underground-hours-before-oct-7/000001a1-a799-d33a-a1e1-efd9865a0000))
