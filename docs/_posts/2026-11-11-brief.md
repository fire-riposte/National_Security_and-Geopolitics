**Bottom line:** British police arrested a senior US Army soldier in England on suspicion of spying for Russia, while Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang faces a November 18 congressional briefing over his ties to a suspected Chinese intelligence asset. Germany's Bundeswehr banned Chinese-made drones and certain Chinese cars from its bases, and the Justice Department unsealed an indictment against three Iran-backed operatives tied to nearly 20 attacks across Europe and the United States. Ukraine's drone campaign has cut Russian oil refining capacity by roughly 30 percent as European officials report a surge in suspected Russian hybrid warfare incidents.

## Espionage and counterintelligence

**UK arrests senior US Army soldier on suspicion of spying for Russia.** British counterterrorism police, working closely with the FBI and US military authorities, arrested an unnamed senior US Army soldier in England on suspicion of assisting Russian intelligence, the Metropolitan Police said. No charges have been announced, and the soldier's unit and rank have not been disclosed. *Why it matters:* an insider-threat case inside the US military presence in Europe, as NATO tries to project a united front against Russia, will draw fresh scrutiny of screening in deployed units. ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/10/uk/us-army-soldier-arrested-uk-alleged-espionage-russia), [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6d7q77xgw0vo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss), [The Record](https://therecord.media/us-army-member-arrested-in-uk-spying-for-russia))

**Congress to question Nvidia's Huang over ties to suspected Chinese spy.** Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has been invited to a House briefing on November 18 about his relationship with Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive US intelligence agencies reportedly assessed was passing information to Beijing, according to the New York Times' anonymous sources. Huang has acknowledged an extramarital affair with her but denies wrongdoing, Nvidia's board says it is investigating, and Alice Huang has not surfaced publicly since the report broke. *Why it matters:* the case has become a proxy fight over how much Washington should trust tech executives to police their own ties to Chinese intelligence efforts, even though the underlying espionage claims remain unproven. ([New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/10/technology/alice-huang-megaspeed-nvidia-jensen-huang.html), [SpyTalk](https://www.spytalk.co/p/where-is-alice-huang))

**Indonesia arrests ex-police chief over alleged wiretapping of the president.** Indonesian police arrested former national police chief Listyo Sigit Prabowo, who had just been named President Prabowo Subianto's chief of staff, along with two senior officers, on suspicion of running unauthorized wiretaps on the president, other officials, politicians and activists between 2025 and 2026. A government minister said the conduct could amount to treason, which carries a maximum penalty of death under Indonesian law. *Why it matters:* a wiretapping scandal implicating the country's top former law-enforcement official threatens to reopen a rift between Prabowo and his predecessor Joko Widodo, to whom the arrested men were close. ([Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2026/11/11/indonesia-wiretap-prabowo-police-chief/), [South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/news/asia/southeast-asia/article/3373180/indonesian-police-arrest-3-tapping-prabowos-phone))

## US military and intelligence community

**Hegseth faces impeachment push over the Iran war.** Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, in his first press conference since May, called an impeachment filing against him "sabotage" and a political "farce," saying criticism of his handling of the Iran war disrespects troops and lays groundwork for prosecuting service members over war-crimes allegations. The push adds to a tense year-end on Capitol Hill, where lawmakers are also still working out major defense spending decisions. *Why it matters:* a sitting defense secretary facing a serious impeachment effort in the middle of an active war and a budget fight shows how contested the administration's Iran policy has become in Washington. ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/video/2026/11/10/hegseth-says-impeachment-filing-against-him-is-sabotage-and-political-farce/), [Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/a-pentagon-impeachment-showdown-plus-defense-funding-in-limbo/))

**US repositions aircraft from UK base amid Iran threat concerns.** The US Air Force has moved at least 12 KC-135 refueling tankers and three CV-22 Osprey aircraft away from RAF Mildenhall in England over the past two days, alongside increased security protocols, amid reports of Iranian threats. *Why it matters:* pulling tankers and special-operations aircraft off a key European base signals Washington is treating a specific, current Iranian threat as serious rather than routine caution. ([The War Zone](https://www.twz.com/air/usaf-aircraft-continue-to-reposition-away-from-raf-mildenhall-amid-reports-of-iranian-threats))

**GAO: Pentagon can't account for millions of F-35 spare parts.** A Government Accountability Office report found the Defense Department struggles to track its F-35 inventory, which includes roughly 26 million aircraft parts worth around $31 billion, even as the F-35 remains the costliest weapons program in US history. *Why it matters:* poor parts accountability on the Pentagon's largest acquisition program raises both readiness risk and the odds that lost or diverted parts go unnoticed. ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/pentagon/2026/11/10/pentagon-cant-track-millions-of-f-35-spare-parts-watchdog-finds/), [USNI News](https://news.usni.org/2026/11/10/gao-report-on-the-defense-departments-f-35-program))

## China, Russia, Iran and North Korea

**US unseals indictment against three Iran-backed operatives over European and US plots.** The Justice Department unsealed an eight-count superseding indictment charging Mohammad Baqer Saad Dawood al-Saadi, Mohsen Mohajeri and Mohammad Ali al-Hassani with terrorism offenses tied to nearly 20 attacks and attempted attacks across Europe and the United States in support of Kata'ib Hezbollah, an Iran-backed militia. Prosecutors allege the plots included an incident near RAF Fairford, the UK airbase used by US forces. *Why it matters:* the case shows Iran-aligned militias running operational cells well beyond the Middle East, reaching into Europe and the US homeland. ([Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/three-iranian-backed-operatives-indicted-their-roles-nearly-20-terrorist-attacks-and), [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c3rryrryk59zo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss))

**Ukraine's drone strikes cut Russian oil refining capacity by about 30 percent.** Ukraine has renewed long-range drone attacks on Russia's energy industry in recent days, with strikes reportedly cutting the country's oil refining capacity by around 30 percent and causing fuel shortages inside Russia, according to the Atlantic Council. *Why it matters:* sustained damage to Russia's refining capacity squeezes both its war economy and its ability to fuel its own military, independent of front-line fighting. ([Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/ukrainealert/ukraines-new-drone-blitz-cuts-russian-oil-refining-capacity-by-30-percent/))

**Europe sees surge in suspected Russian hybrid warfare incidents.** European officials have logged a spike in suspected Russian sabotage, drone incursions and other hybrid-warfare incidents in recent weeks, which analysts say reflects an escalating intimidation campaign against Ukraine's European backers, according to the Atlantic Council. *Why it matters:* a rising pattern of below-threshold attacks pressures European governments to respond without triggering a wider confrontation with Moscow. ([Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/ukrainealert/europe-sees-surge-in-suspected-russian-hybrid-warfare-incidents/))

**China publicly unveils GJ-11 stealth combat drone.** China showcased its GJ-11 "Sharp Sword" flying-wing stealth combat drone, demonstrating strike and manned-unmanned teaming capabilities that had previously only been glimpsed in leaked images. *Why it matters:* an open reveal of a stealth strike drone built to team with crewed fighters signals Beijing wants rivals to register how far its unmanned combat programs have advanced. ([The War Zone](https://www.twz.com/air/china-lifts-the-veil-on-the-gj-11-mysterious-dragon-stealth-ucav))

## Germany and Israel

**Bundeswehr bars Chinese drones and certain Chinese-made cars from its bases.** Germany's Spiegel magazine reported that the Bundeswehr has banned small Chinese-made drones from barracks and training grounds, with a defense ministry spokesman saying the military will rely on national or European products instead; the military has also barred cars from Chinese brands including BYD and Geely from parking on base, reportedly over concern that onboard cameras and sensors could leak data to Chinese authorities. *Why it matters:* it puts Germany alongside Israel, Poland and the UK in treating everyday Chinese-made hardware on military sites as a tangible espionage risk rather than a hypothetical one. ([t-online, in German](https://t-online.de/nachrichten/deutschland/innenpolitik/id_101522616/bundeswehr-verbannt-wohl-chinesische-drohnen-und-autos-aus-kasernen.html), [Stuttgarter Zeitung, in German](https://stuttgarter-zeitung.de/wissen/sorge-vor-spionage-bundeswehr-welche-chinesischen-autos-duerfen-nicht-in-kasernen-79689709.html))

**Bundeswehr seeks €3 billion for satellite-based missile warning system.** Germany's defense ministry wants roughly 3 billion euro in additional funding over the coming years for a satellite system to give early warning of hostile ballistic missile launches, according to a revised proposal for the 2027 budget reported by Augen geradeaus. *Why it matters:* it's a concrete step toward Germany building its own independent missile early-warning capability rather than relying solely on allied systems. ([Augen geradeaus!, in German](https://augengeradeaus.net/2026/11/bundeswehr-plant-drei-milliarden-euro-fuer-satelliten-gestuetztes-raketenwarnsystem/))

**Israeli police find Shin Bet's evidence against Arab lawmaker insufficient.** Israel Police concluded that evidence the Shin Bet domestic security service presented, alleging the Palestinian Authority funded former Joint List leader Ayman Odeh, was insufficient to even question him under caution, with a senior source saying the material raised "more question marks than exclamation marks." *Why it matters:* a domestic security agency's politically sensitive allegation against a sitting Arab lawmaker not holding up under police review raises questions about how the Shin Bet handles evidence in politically charged cases. ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/israel-politics/2026-11-10/ty-article/.premium/police-unimpressed-with-shin-bets-evidence-that-arab-mk-received-funds-from-pa/000001a1-c71d-dc22-abe1-ff7dd6a00000))
