**Bottom line:** MI5 publicly named a Chinese front institute recruiting UK academics for Beijing's intelligence services, while the FBI said it's hunting the criminal group that stole its own employees' data and Latvia detained a couple accused of passing NATO and defense intelligence to Russia's military intelligence agency. In the Gulf, Mossad and Shin Bet opened a terrorism investigation into an apparent attempt to crash an Israel-bound flight, and the UK said there are "strong indications" Iran was behind a separate plot against a US-operated airbase.

## Espionage and counterintelligence

**MI5 names a Chinese institute as an espionage front, tells academics to cut ties.** MI5 publicly identified the China General Technology Research Institute (CGTRI), a state-owned conglomerate with deep ties to China's Ministry of State Security, saying it funded research from more than 100 UK-linked academics in fields including artificial intelligence and covert communications that directly builds Beijing's espionage capability. Security Minister Dan Jarvis urged academics to end all arrangements with CGTRI immediately. *Why it matters:* It's an unusually direct move to name and dismantle a specific Chinese intelligence-linked research network rather than issue a general warning. ([inews.co.uk](https://inews.co.uk/news/mi5-universities-beijing-chinese-spies-4799675))

**FBI vows to hunt down the hackers who stole its employees' data.** The bureau's cyber division assistant director Brett Leatherman publicly challenged the ShinyHunters group, which claims to have stolen 2 to 3 terabytes of data including FBI employees' and applicants' home addresses, spouses' names and medical records, telling them "you know how to find us, and we know how to find you." The FBI says it's still investigating how the hackers got the data, including whether they breached a vendor or the bureau's own systems. *Why it matters:* A breach of the FBI's own workforce records is a serious counterintelligence exposure, handing adversaries a roadmap to bureau personnel. ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/30/nx-s1-5985202/fbi-hack-shinyhunters))

**A decade-long Chinese operation to recruit a Fed economist ends in conviction.** A CNBC investigation detailed how a Chinese intelligence officer using the alias "Hummin Lee" spent ten years cultivating former Federal Reserve economist John Rogers, including arranging a romantic relationship with a much younger woman, to extract information from inside the Fed; Rogers was convicted of lying to investigators, acquitted of economic espionage, and sentenced to 38 months in prison. *Why it matters:* The case shows Chinese intelligence services will run yearslong personal-relationship operations against mid-level officials with access to sensitive economic policy, not just senior officials. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/30/john-rogers-fed-china-espionage-case.html))

**Russia's domestic security service opens a terrorism case against a London exile leader.** The FSB said it launched a criminal investigation on terrorism charges against Ksenia Maximova, who leads the Russian Democratic Society UK, days after the US revealed a separate Russian plot to assassinate dissidents in Washington; the FSB claimed it would keep "neutralizing" what it called UK-directed activity by Russian exile groups. *Why it matters:* Moscow routinely alleges exile groups are foreign intelligence fronts to justify pursuing critics abroad, and this is the second such campaign disclosed in two weeks. ([CEPA](https://cepa.org/article/fsb-focuses-crosshairs-on-russian-exiles/))

**Latvia detains a couple accused of spying on NATO forces for Russia's GRU.** Latvia's State Security Service and military intelligence detained two Latvian citizens in June on suspicion of passing Russia's military intelligence agency, the GRU, information on a national air base, radar systems and NATO troop movements, including aerial photography of the Ādaži military base from a private aircraft. *Why it matters:* The case adds to a string of Baltic GRU recruitment cases targeting people with access to installations hosting NATO forces. ([LSM](https://eng.lsm.lv/article/society/crime/01.10.2026-couple-in-latvia-suspected-of-spying-for-russia.a665602/), [BNN](https://bnn-news.com/social/collected-intelligence-on-latvian-military-and-nato-vdd-detains-couple-suspected-of-spying-for-russia-rrmxn4p))

**Suspected Chinese hackers posed as a former White House tech official to target AI policy experts.** Cybersecurity firm Proofpoint said a group it tracks as TA419 impersonated Lynne Parker, a former senior White House technology official, to email a US law firm inviting it to a fake "AI policy advisory committee," part of a campaign against experts on AI export controls and military AI at universities, think tanks and law firms. Proofpoint attributed the activity to China based on malware and infrastructure but found no confirmed breaches. *Why it matters:* Beijing's intelligence services are treating US AI policy expertise as a priority collection target as the two countries compete on AI capability. ([Nextgov](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/10/china-linked-hackers-posed-former-us-officials-anthropic-employee-target-ai-experts/416356/))

## US military and intelligence community

**DIA built a new framework to measure a military's "will to fight" after misjudging Ukraine.** The Defense Intelligence Agency adopted a new analytical model after underestimating Ukrainian forces' resistance to Russia's 2022 invasion, aiming to put a more rigorous methodology behind the previously subjective judgment of whether a foreign military will actually fight. *Why it matters:* Getting this call wrong shapes US assessments of future conflicts, including a potential fight over Taiwan. ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/09/exclusive-how-the-dia-measures-a-militarys-will-to-fight/))

**Pentagon puts Cyber Command leadership on notice after a cluster of suicide deaths.** An August 31 memo from the Pentagon's assistant secretary for cyber policy made specific demands of US Cyber Command leadership following reports of a cluster of suicide deaths among its personnel. *Why it matters:* The intervention signals the Pentagon sees a command-specific problem inside one of its most sensitive units, not just a broader military mental-health trend. ([The Record](https://therecord.media/cyber-command-suicide-deaths-pentagon-memo))

**White House names a nominee to lead Space Command amid its headquarters move.** The administration nominated Lt. Gen. David Miller to lead US Space Command (SPACECOM), putting him in charge of the ongoing relocation of its headquarters from Colorado to Alabama, along with a nominee for the Navy's No. 2 post. *Why it matters:* Leadership turnover at Space Command comes as the relocation fight has drawn political scrutiny over operational readiness. ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/09/white-house-names-nominees-for-spacecom-navy-no-2/))

## China, Russia, Iran and North Korea

**China opens a third overseas military outpost, an air force training center in Laos.** China established an air force support training center north of Vientiane, a counterpart to a naval facility it runs in Cambodia, marking its third overseas military outpost; satellite imagery shows the facility built partly on a former US airstrip from the Vietnam War. *Why it matters:* Each new overseas outpost expands China's ability to project power and gather intelligence beyond its borders, even without a formal base agreement. ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/09/china-opens-training-center-with-laos-marking-third-overseas-military-base/), [The Diplomat](https://thediplomat.com/2026/10/chinas-military-open-new-logistics-and-training-facility-in-laos/))

**Russia hits Ukraine's power grid in its largest attack since spring.** Russia struck 11 regions of Ukraine overnight, hitting a drone base, an oil facility and military logistics along with residential buildings and railways, in what Ukraine's prime minister called the largest assault on energy infrastructure since spring. *Why it matters:* Moscow is returning to systematic strikes on civilian energy infrastructure as winter approaches, a tactic aimed at public morale as much as military capability. ([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ckjw5jx2l393o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss))

**UK says there are "strong indications" Iran was behind a plot against a US-operated airbase.** Five British men in their 20s were arrested after police intercepted three vehicles carrying petrol heading toward RAF Fairford, the UK base the US has used to launch strikes on Iranian missile sites; UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham said there are strong indications of Iranian involvement, though investigators haven't ruled out Russia or a non-state group, and Iran has denied any role. *Why it matters:* An alleged plot against a base used for strikes on Iran would mark a major escalation of Iran-linked activity on British soil. ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/30/uk/suspect-police-terrorism-uk-base-fairford-intl-hnk))

## Germany and Israel

**Bundeswehr rehearses the NATO defense plan built to move 800,000 allied troops through Germany.** At the end of September, the Bundeswehr tested its classified "Operationsplan Deutschland," a roughly 1,400-page logistics plan for hosting, supplying, protecting and moving NATO forces through Germany in a war with Russia, with the Bundeswehr's Operational Command coordinating between the military, states, authorities and businesses. *Why it matters:* Because Germany's central location makes it NATO's logistics hub, the exercise tests whether the country's infrastructure and institutions can deliver during an actual crisis, not just on paper. ([Telepolis, in German](https://www.telepolis.de/article/Deutschland-plant-fuer-den-Ernstfall-Kann-die-Infrastruktur-halten-11095546.html))

**Estonia's intelligence chief says Russian sabotage against European arms makers, including in Germany, is "systematic."** Margo Palloson, head of Estonia's Internal Security Service, said Russia is increasingly targeting European defense companies with sabotage and cyberattacks, citing a foiled explosive-laden-drone attack at Germany's Leipzig Airport and an arson attack Estonia blames on Russia at defense manufacturer Milrem Robotics, which builds unmanned systems used in Ukraine. He urged all suppliers to Ukraine's war effort to strengthen their cyberdefenses. *Why it matters:* A sitting intelligence chief naming Germany-based targets points to a widening Russian campaign against the industrial base arming Ukraine. ([ERR News](https://news.err.ee/1610151220/estonia-russia-responsible-for-arson-attack-on-defence-contractor-milrem))

**Mossad and Shin Bet join investigation into apparent attempt to crash an Israel-bound flight.** A co-pilot identified as an Omani national stabbed the captain and apparently tried to crash flydubai flight FZ1073 from Dubai to Tel Aviv before passengers and the injured captain overpowered him and the plane diverted to Saudi Arabia; Israel has formally classified the incident as terrorism and opened a joint investigation involving Mossad, Shin Bet, the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) and the Transport Ministry, though officials say they have not found clear evidence Iran directed the plot. *Why it matters:* Investigators are focused on how a pilot from a country with no diplomatic ties to Israel ended up flying a plane full of Israelis, a vetting question with implications beyond this flight. ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/middle-east/flight-dubai-tel-aviv-diverted-emergency-alert-rcna600628))
