**Bottom line:** Germany arrested its own former foreign intelligence chief on treason charges, days after a separate Iranian espionage case prompted Berlin to summon Tehran's ambassador. Chinese state-backed hackers are exploiting a zero-day in widely used remote-support software, and the CIA director reportedly traveled to Moscow to warn Russia off testing NATO even as President Trump suspended new sanctions on Russia over objections from Kyiv.

## Espionage and counterintelligence

**Germany arrests its own former spy chief on treason charges.** German federal prosecutors arrested August Hanning, who led the Bundesnachrichtendienst (BND), Germany's foreign intelligence service, from 1998 to 2005, on suspicion of treason and acting as a foreign agent. Prosecutors allege Hanning's former chief of staff handed him roughly 2,000 classified documents after Hanning left office, which he then used in his private consulting work, and investigators are still trying to determine whether any of that material reached a foreign intelligence service. *Why it matters:* A former chief of Germany's main spy agency facing treason charges is a serious blow to the BND's credibility and raises questions about oversight of retired officials who keep access to sensitive material.
([Tagesspiegel](https://www.tagesspiegel.de/wegen-spionageverdachts-ex-bnd-chef-august-hanning-verhaftet-16130486.html), [infranken.de](https://www.infranken.de/deutschland/ex-bnd-praesident-hanning-wegen-spionageverdacht-festgenommen-art-6401159))

**Berlin summons Iran's ambassador over an alleged spy network targeting Jewish sites.** Germany's federal prosecutor's office had a 53-year-old man of Afghan origin arrested in Denmark on suspicion of spying on Jewish institutions in Berlin for Iran's Revolutionary Guard, and extradition proceedings are underway. Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul summoned Iran's ambassador and said the allegations, if confirmed, would show again that Iran is a danger to Jews worldwide; Iran's embassy called the accusations "unfounded and dangerous."  *Why it matters:* It is the latest in a string of German cases tying Iranian intelligence to surveillance of Jewish and Israeli targets in Europe.
([Jüdische Allgemeine](https://www.juedische-allgemeine.de/politik/aussenminister-wadephul-bestellt-iranischen-botschafter-ein/))

**Chinese state hackers exploited a zero-day in remote-support software to break into targets worldwide.** Google and Mandiant say a Chinese government-backed group tracked as Armory Castle, or UNC5221, exploited a critical zero-day in BeyondTrust's Remote Support product starting in late August, before the vendor patched it on October 8. The group has a history of burrowing into edge devices from vendors including Ivanti and Citrix, and researchers confirmed at least one compromised BeyondTrust customer with more likely affected. *Why it matters:* Edge devices and remote-support software are a favored entry point for Chinese espionage operations because they sit outside normal endpoint monitoring.
([CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/china-espionage-attacks-beyondtrust-zero-day-armory-castle/), [The Record](https://therecord.media/beyond-trust-cyberattack-china))

**Moscow police arrest suspected members of a hacking group that stole hundreds of millions of records.** Russian authorities detained a 19-year-old and placed him under house arrest, with two more suspects reportedly in custody, in connection with DataSuckers, a group accused of breaching the Dodo Pizza chain and attempting to extort a Ukrainian grocery chain and a Russian tour operator. One suspect was allegedly traced after exposing his own IP address while accessing stolen data. *Why it matters:* The case shows Russian police will move against financially motivated hackers even when their targets include Ukrainian companies.
([The Record](https://therecord.media/russia-datasuckers-hackers-arrested))

**US charges a California woman with spying on Taiwan's president's family for China.** The FBI arrested Wanying Zhang, 34, at Los Angeles International Airport as she prepared to fly to China, accusing her of acting as an unregistered Chinese agent. Prosecutors say Zhang traveled to Seattle in 2025 on instructions from Chinese officials to surveil the home of Lai Ting-yu, the son of Taiwan's president, and sent photos, video and license-plate data back to her handler. *Why it matters:* Taiwan's government called it transnational repression, and the case adds to a run of Chinese intelligence operations targeting the relatives of Taiwanese officials on US soil.
([KMPH](https://kmph.com/news/nation-world/fbi-arrests-california-woman-accused-of-spying-on-taiwan-leaders-family-for-china))

**A defected Russian officer says he fed Ukraine targeting data that helped kill his own soldiers.** The Wall Street Journal reported, as relayed by Russian and Ukrainian outlets, that Lieutenant Lev Stupnikov, a signals officer in Russia's 36th Motorized Rifle Brigade, passed coordinates and radio frequencies to Ukrainian intelligence for months, including information those outlets say helped Ukraine kill more than 200 Russian soldiers, among them a strike near Vuhledar that killed the brigade commander. Stupnikov fled across the front line in July 2024 after Russian command suspected a leak, and has since joined a Russian opposition unit fighting alongside Ukraine's military. *Why it matters:* The case points to deep Ukrainian intelligence penetration of Russian frontline units more than three years into the war.
([Zona Media](https://zona.media/news/2026/10/16/stupnikov))

## US military and intelligence community

**CIA director reportedly traveled to Moscow to warn Russia off testing NATO.** The Wall Street Journal and Politico reported that CIA Director John Ratcliffe made a previously undisclosed trip to Moscow to tell Russian officials not to attack any NATO member, reportedly naming Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania, and to press Russia to stop backing Iran militarily. President Trump called the visit "semi-routine," and the Kremlin confirmed meetings with Russian intelligence officials but said Vladimir Putin was not involved, dismissing talk of an attack on NATO as baseless. *Why it matters:* The reported warning reflects US intelligence assessments that Putin could test the alliance's resolve with a limited strike on a member state within the next few years.
([Mediaite](https://www.mediaite.com/politics/cia-director-reportedly-traveled-to-moscow-to-warn-russia-not-to-attack-nato-countries/), [Al-Ahram English](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/575375.aspx))

**Justice Department agrees to drop its case against a former CIA analyst accused of working for South Korea.** Prosecutors filed a deferred prosecution agreement for Sue Mi Terry, charged in 2024 with failing to register as a foreign agent and sharing government information with South Korean intelligence officials in exchange for gifts including designer handbags. Under the deal, the charges are dropped after a year if she avoids legal trouble and limits her contact with the South Korean government; her attorney said she always maintained her contacts served US interests. *Why it matters:* Dropping a case this close to trial, without public explanation, raises questions about how strong the original charges were.
([Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/world/20261015/us-drops-case-against-ex-cia-analyst-accused-of-aiding-s-korea))

**FBI turmoil deepens as fired agents sue over dismissals tied to Trump-related cases.** SpyTalk reports FBI agents are suing over their firing by FBI Director Kash Patel, in cases that include several agents who worked on the classified-documents investigation into Trump; separately, former acting FBI Director Brian Driscoll described, in an interview, being seen internally as having protected colleagues by refusing to turn over the names of agents who worked Trump-related cases. *Why it matters:* The firings and lawsuits point to a bureau still absorbing political pressure over cases tied to the president, with implications for morale and independence in ongoing investigations.
([SpyTalk](https://www.spytalk.co/p/new-in-spyweek-starlink-chaos-ukraine), [SpyTalk](https://www.spytalk.co/p/inside-the-fbi-firings-with-former))

## China, Russia, Iran and North Korea

**Trump suspends new Russia sanctions, drawing a warning from Zelenskyy.** President Trump blocked a round of sanctions on Russia two days before they were due to take effect, saying the move was meant to keep peace negotiations alive. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned the waiver could help Putin and risks pressuring Ukraine toward a forced capitulation. *Why it matters:* Easing sanctions pressure on Moscow while fighting continues signals a shift in US leverage over any eventual settlement, and Europe is now weighing how to respond.
([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c9p8gz7zx4peo))

**A second Chinese submarine drone mothership has surfaced.** The War Zone reports a scaled-down version of China's first known submarine-launched drone mothership has emerged, built by Guangzhou Shipyard International, a yard known for specialized naval vessels. *Why it matters:* A second hull suggests China intends to field unmanned underwater systems at scale rather than as a one-off prototype.
([The War Zone](https://www.twz.com/sea/second-chinese-submarine-drone-mothership-emerges))

**Iran nominates a UN-sanctioned missile industry veteran as defense minister.** President Masoud Pezeshkian nominated Mehrdad Akhlaghi Ketabchi, a figure under United Nations (UN) and US sanctions since 2007 and 2020 respectively for heading Iran's Shahid Bagheri Industrial Group, which supports its ballistic missile program. Iranian lawmakers described the pick as a message to Iran's adversaries; the post has been vacant since Iran's previous defense minister was killed in US-Israeli strikes in February. *Why it matters:* The choice signals Tehran plans to keep prioritizing missile development as it rebuilds its military leadership after the war with Israel.
([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/mena/pezeshkian-nominates-sanctions-listed-mehrdad-akhlaghi-ketabchi-as-iran-defence-minister-1.500699695))

**A senior IRGC commander has been dispatched to Lebanon to shore up Hezbollah.** The Alma Center reports Hossein Yekta, a commander in Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) linked to recruitment and ideological work, arrived in Lebanon this month as part of a delegation supporting Hezbollah families. *Why it matters:* The deployment points to Iran actively working to rebuild Hezbollah's cohesion after a bruising war with Israel.
([Alma Center](https://israel-alma.org/hossein-yekta-senior-irgc-official-sent-to-lebanon-to-strengthen-hezbollah/))

## Germany and Israel

**Germany's military gains its own dedicated radar-satellite access.** The Bundeswehr began operating under SPOCK 1, a roughly 1.7 billion euro program with Rheinmetall and ICEYE, giving German forces direct tasking rights over several synthetic-aperture radar satellites as of October 1, on top of existing access to the wider constellation. The capability is being used mainly to monitor NATO's eastern flank and support Germany's brigade in Lithuania, with full operational capability expected next year. *Why it matters:* Dedicated satellite tasking reduces Germany's reliance on allies for reconnaissance along Russia's borders, a capability gap NATO has pushed members to close.
([Rheinmetall](https://www.rheinmetall.com/en/media/news-watch/news/2026/10/2026-10-06-rheinmetall-iceye-space-solutions-provides-the-bundeswehr-with-access-to-sar-satellites))

**Israel Defense Forces (IDF) and Shin Bet arrest 10 suspects over an attack on a US journalist and European Union (EU) diplomats.** Israeli security forces, including Border Police and IDF units, arrested 10 people in connection with settler attacks on a US journalist and European diplomats in the West Bank that drew international condemnation. The arrests follow separate Haaretz reporting that the IDF's emergency hotline for such incidents has repeatedly gone unanswered. *Why it matters:* The case has become a test of whether Israel will hold settlers accountable for violence against foreign nationals amid diplomatic pressure from Washington and Europe.
([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/israel-crime/2026-10-19/ty-article/.premium/ten-arrested-following-settler-attacks-on-european-diplomats-press-photographer/000001a1-52c2-d6fb-a1e9-7efa96690000))

**Researchers say an Israeli ad-tech firm's platform enabled spyware makers to surveil Al Jazeera and Palestinian sites.** Citizen Lab found that Discover-Tech's advertising technology reached more than 120,000 websites and 75,000 apps, giving surveillance firms, including the US-blacklisted Candiru, a channel to track visitors to Al Jazeera and Palestinian outlets. *Why it matters:* The finding shows how commercial ad-tech, not just state spy agencies, can be repurposed as surveillance infrastructure against journalists.
([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/security-aviation/2026-10-17/ty-article/israeli-firm-exploited-online-ads-to-spy-on-al-jazeera-and-palestinian-sites/000001a1-4931-d358-a7b5-fb3b34df0000))
