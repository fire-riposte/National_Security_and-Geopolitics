**Bottom line:** Germany's Bundeswehr rated the risk of Russian sabotage against its forces as "considerable" for the first time, citing a likely intent to undercut German support for Ukraine, while British police rearrested two men in the Iran-linked RAF Fairford spy case. Chinese forces tracked a US warship near a contested South China Sea shoal, and Russia warned NATO it could respond with nuclear weapons over Kaliningrad. In Israel, Netanyahu warned Iran may try to disrupt the October 27 election as the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) and Shin Bet kept striking Hamas's internal security structure.

## Espionage and counterintelligence

**Bundeswehr rates Russian sabotage threat "considerable" for the first time.** Germany's armed forces classified the risk of Russian sabotage against the Bundeswehr as "erheblich" (considerable), the second-highest tier on its scale, according to an internal assessment reported by German media. The assessment points to recent drone sightings near Leipzig/Halle airport and the Bundeswehr site at Wunstorf, and says the likely aim is "Zersetzungsabsicht," intent to subvert German political and public support for Ukraine. *Why it matters:* It's the clearest German military acknowledgment yet that Russian sabotage, not just espionage, is treated as a live threat to the armed forces.
([Audimax.de, in German](https://audimax.de/politik/wahrscheinlich-zersetzungsabsicht-bundeswehr-warnt-vor-erheblicher-gefahr-durch-russische-sabotage/), [Stuttgarter Zeitung, in German](https://www.stuttgarter-zeitung.de/politik/geheimbericht-bundeswehr-sieht-sich-von-russland-bedroht-79593662.html))

**UK rearrests two men in RAF Fairford case linked to Iran.** British counter-terrorism police rearrested a 24-year-old British national and a 25-year-old dual British-Iranian national, both from Westminster, on suspicion of assisting a foreign intelligence service, as part of the investigation into suspicious vans found near the US-run RAF Fairford air base on September 27. Police say it is "highly likely" the activity involved Iranian state involvement; five other men arrested in the case remain on bail and no one has been charged. *Why it matters:* It shows UK authorities still treat the alleged Iran-linked plot near a US airbase as an active case, not a closed one.
([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/20/uk/two-men-arrested-iran-uk-base-fairford-intl), [CBS News](https://cbsnews.com/news/raf-fairford-security-incident-arrests-suspected-spying-iran))

**Russian GRU hackers breach NATO shipbuilder and aircraft engine maker.** Security researchers at Symantec said the Russian military intelligence-linked hacking group APT28, also known as Fancy Bear, breached a shipbuilder, an aircraft engine maker and a law enforcement agency in two NATO member countries in an espionage campaign. It's the same group blamed for hacking Democratic Party emails during the 2016 US presidential election. *Why it matters:* It's a continued sign Russian military intelligence is targeting the defense industrial base that supplies NATO members.
([CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/russian-hackers-apt28-target-nato-defense-firms/))

**Chinese nationals charged with spying on Korea-US military drills.** South Korean authorities charged two Chinese nationals, both former Chinese military servicemen, with espionage: one allegedly set up radio-interception equipment near Gunsan air base to intercept South Korean and US fighter pilot and air-traffic-control communications since 2024, while the other allegedly used a relationship with a woman working at Camp Humphreys to obtain classified information on joint exercises, including Ulchi Freedom Shield. Both face charges of aiding an enemy state, which carry a maximum sentence of life in prison; China's foreign ministry said it was not familiar with the situation. *Why it matters:* It raises concern in Seoul and Washington about how easily shared intelligence practices around joint drills can be compromised.
([The Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10880119))

**Report estimates Russian hostile activity costs Britain up to £2.5 billion a year.** A report by Labour MP Graeme Downie and researcher Dominic Reed, titled "The Putin Tax," estimates Russian hostile activity costs the UK £2 billion to £2.5 billion a year, built from specific incidents like the Royal Mail cyberattack, an arson attack in Leyton and the Jaguar Land Rover hack, plus an assumption that Russia is behind roughly a tenth of the UK's annual cybercrime losses. Downie raised the estimate in the House of Commons, and the prime minister said he would look at better ways to track the cost. *Why it matters:* It puts a concrete price tag on a diffuse threat, though the figure rests on an assumption about Russia's share of UK cybercrime, not precise attribution.
([The Record](https://therecord.media/russia-cyberattacks-on-britain-putin-tax-graeme-downie))

## US military and intelligence community

**US-led Arctic experiment tests ways to close air-surveillance gaps.** US military personnel, scientists and international partners carried out live-fly testing in Alaska in the final event of a three-year series of experiments on Arctic air-domain awareness, gathering data on technology meant to close gaps in surveillance coverage across the state. *Why it matters:* As Russia and China increase activity in the Arctic, Washington is trying to fix blind spots in its ability to track aircraft over the region.
([Department of War](https://www.war.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/4629316/arctic-experiment-explores-ways-to-close-air-surveillance-gaps/))

**Pentagon plans to expand bases in Greenland next year.** The Pentagon intends to begin expanding its military bases in Greenland next year, with upgrades to support cargo and fighter aircraft and additional troops, a defense official said. *Why it matters:* It's a concrete step behind the Trump administration's stated interest in a bigger US military foothold in Greenland, which sits astride Arctic shipping routes and missile-warning approaches.
([Defense One](https://www.defenseone.com/policy/2026/10/pentagon-expand-greenland-bases/416706/))

**Pentagon weighs cost of rebuilding Middle East base posture.** A senior defense official said the Pentagon is working with Central Command on assessments of what its installation footprint in the Middle East should look like going forward, as it weighs the cost of rebuilding bases there. *Why it matters:* The review signals the US is rethinking how much permanent infrastructure it wants in the region after years of strain from the Gaza war and strikes on Iran.
([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/pentagon-weighs-force-posture-costs-of-rebuilding-middle-east-bases/))

## China, Russia, Iran and North Korea

**Chinese forces track US warship near contested Sabina Shoal.** Chinese naval and other forces tracked the US littoral combat ship USS Charleston on Tuesday after it sailed near Sabina Shoal, a contested feature in the South China Sea, with Beijing saying the US ship had "illegally intruded" into the surrounding waters. *Why it matters:* It's the latest in a steady pattern of close shadowing between US and Chinese forces around contested South China Sea features claimed by Beijing and the Philippines.
([USNI News](https://news.usni.org/2026/10/20/chinese-navy-shadows-uss-charleston-near-contested-sabina-shoal))

**Russia warns of nuclear response over Kaliningrad.** Russia's Foreign Ministry sent NATO a diplomatic note in late September threatening to use nuclear weapons if the alliance tries to "isolate" Russia's Kaliningrad exclave, following a series of military moves around the Baltic enclave. *Why it matters:* It's an unusually explicit nuclear threat tied to a specific piece of territory, raising the stakes of an already tense Baltic security standoff.
([Jamestown Foundation](https://jamestown.org/putin-makes-nuclear-threats-over-kaliningrad/))

**Report: Trump opposed Netanyahu's plan for new strikes on Iran.** The Financial Times reported, according to Haaretz, that Israel had planned strikes on Iran's power plants, nuclear facilities and regime targets in early October, in a campaign more intense than the one it launched in February, but that Trump opposed the plan. *Why it matters:* The reported disagreement points to continued US-Israeli friction over how far to escalate with Iran.
([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/middle-east-news/iran/2026-10-21/ty-article/report-trump-opposed-netanyahus-proposed-early-october-strikes-on-iran/000001a1-5cd6-d99f-a3e5-7df7c3350000))

**Japan reports surge in Chinese and Russian airspace violations.** Japan's Air Self-Defense Force scrambled fighter jets 442 times between April 1 and September 30 in response to airspace violations by Chinese and Russian aircraft, a sharp rise after a decrease in 2025, according to Japanese defense data. *Why it matters:* The jump points to intensifying Chinese and Russian military probing of Japanese airspace even as both countries publicly emphasize stability with Tokyo.
([USNI News](https://news.usni.org/2026/10/20/japan-reports-surge-in-airspace-violations-by-chinese-russian-aircraft))

## Germany and Israel

**IDF and Shin Bet kill senior Hamas security official in Gaza City.** The IDF and Shin Bet said they killed Akram Bassam Shaaban Yassin, a senior official in Hamas's General Security Mechanism, a covert apparatus Israel says protects the group's leadership, in an airstrike in the Gaza City area. The military linked the strike to an earlier round of strikes roughly four months ago that killed other officials in the same unit. *Why it matters:* It shows Israel is still conducting targeted strikes against Hamas's internal security structure months after the ceasefire took hold.
([Israel National News](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/434912))

**Netanyahu warns Iran may try to disrupt Israel's October 27 election.** Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu told Army Radio that Israel was prepared for war with Iran and said there would likely be an attempt by Iran, directly or through proxies such as Hamas or Hezbollah, to interfere in the October 27 election, without disclosing the underlying intelligence. Opposition leader Yair Lapid said after a security briefing there was "no reason to panic," and defense officials said they had not identified a specific imminent threat. *Why it matters:* The gap between Netanyahu's public warning and officials' more measured assessment shows how contested election-security claims have become in Israel's current political climate.
([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/israel-politics/2026-10-20/ty-article/netanyahu-says-iran-trying-to-interfere-in-israels-elections/000001a1-5a56-d014-afa7-fbf700d90000), [The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/pm-iran-working-to-orchestrate-attack-to-influence-election-maybe-through-hamas-and-hezbollah/))
