**Bottom line:** Germany arrested its former BND chief, August Hanning, on treason and espionage charges, one of the most serious cases to hit German intelligence in years. NPR reports FBI counterintelligence squads are running at little more than half strength even as China, Russia and Iran keep up active espionage operations against the US, and Axios reports the Pentagon has been told to prepare for a possible resumption of strikes on Iran before the midterms.

## Espionage and counterintelligence

**Germany's former spy chief arrested on treason and espionage charges.** Prosecutors arrested August Hanning, who led the BND, Germany's foreign intelligence service, from 1998 to 2005, on October 6 on suspicion of treasonous espionage and attempted treason. Investigators allege his former chief of staff, identified only as Manfred D., supplied him with roughly 2,000 classified documents over the years, which Hanning used to prepare analysis for an officer of an unnamed foreign intelligence service; German media say the case does not involve Russia. *Why it matters:* It is one of the most serious espionage cases to hit German intelligence in decades and raises fresh questions about oversight of retired officials with ongoing access to classified material.
([Courthouse News](https://courthousenews.com/ex-spy-chief-is-arrested-in-germany-on-suspicion-of-trading-state-secrets-and-treason/), [EUobserver](https://euobserver.com/241332/german-ex-spy-chief-arrested-over-espionage-and-treason-allegations-involving-leaked-bnd-files/), [GBA, in German](https://www.presseportal.de/blaulicht/pm/14981/6366753))

**Ex-CIA official who stole $40 million in gold also exposed a US source's identity.** David Rush, a former senior CIA official, pleaded guilty to wire fraud after FBI agents found more than 300 gold bars, roughly $2 million in cash and dozens of luxury watches at his Virginia home, bought using a fabricated "classified" program. Prosecutors said Rush also gave a foreign government official information that identified a secret US intelligence source. *Why it matters:* A CIA officer trading away a human source's identity for personal gain is a counterintelligence failure layered on top of the fraud.
([KSAT](https://www.ksat.com/news/politics/2026/10/06/former-cia-official-accused-of-stealing-40m-in-gold-bars-pleads-guilty/), [NBC Miami](https://www.nbcmiami.com/news/national-international/cia-david-rush-secret-intelligence-asset-foreign-official/3869258/))

**FBI charges two California figures accused of spying for China.** The FBI arrested Irvine real estate agent Wanying "Heather" Zhang at Los Angeles International Airport as she prepared to board a flight to Shanghai, charging her with acting as an unregistered agent of a foreign government; prosecutors allege she surveilled the family of Taiwanese President Lai Ching-te's son in the Seattle area last year and passed along license plate numbers and other details to contacts linked to Beijing. Arcadia Mayor Eileen Wang was separately charged with acting as an illegal foreign agent. *Why it matters:* The cases show Beijing using people embedded in ordinary professions and local politics, not just trained intelligence officers, to track and pressure its targets abroad.
([AsiaNews Network](https://asianews.network/?p=305931), [KY3](https://www.ky3.com/2026/10/05/california-woman-arrested-allegedly-spying-china/))

**FBI counterintelligence squads are running far under strength.** NPR reports that in many of the FBI's 56 field offices, squads dedicated to tracking foreign spies are filled to only 50 to 60 percent of authorized positions, with the number of squads doing the work cut in half in multiple offices; the bureau says the cuts were not ordered by Director Kash Patel but came as resources shifted toward immigration enforcement. *Why it matters:* The cuts land as China, Russia and Iran all keep running active intelligence operations against the US, according to officials NPR cited.
([NPR via St. Louis Public Radio](https://www.stlpr.org/npr/2026-10-06/fbis-spy-hunting-squads-hit-with-deep-cuts-even-as-foreign-espionage-threats-soar))

## US military and intelligence community

**Pentagon launches AI pilot to manage classified information.** The Department of War's acquisition and sustainment directorate issued a memo describing a pilot program that will use artificial intelligence to manage sensitive and classified information, framing it as a way to cut "dysfunction" in outdated classification and declassification procedures. *Why it matters:* Automating classification decisions could speed information sharing, but it also creates a new point of failure if the system misclassifies or exposes sensitive material.
([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/10/07/pentagon-launches-ai-pilot-program-automated-classification-management/))

**Army activates new sustainment brigade for the Pacific.** The Army stood up a new sustainment brigade as part of a restructuring of its Pacific posture, a move that will send additional soldiers to Okinawa, Japan. *Why it matters:* It is a small but concrete step toward the logistics backbone the Army says it would need in a Pacific contingency.
([Defense One](https://www.defenseone.com/threats/2026/10/army-activates-sustainment-brigade-part-pacific-restructuring/416486/))

## China, Russia, Iran and North Korea

**Pentagon told to prepare for possible renewed strikes on Iran.** Axios reported the US military has been ordered to prepare for a possible resumption of large-scale operations against Iran, and Haaretz adds that Pentagon planners are weighing options as Washington tallies the cost of the earlier war alongside tight midterm congressional races; Vice President JD Vance said any new nuclear deal requires concrete steps from Tehran first. *Why it matters:* A decision to strike again would land in an election season, raising the stakes of any miscalculation.
([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/middle-east-news/2026-10-08/ty-article/report-trump-weighing-iran-strike-before-midterms-amid-soaring-energy-costs/000001a1-1950-d70d-a5b9-fbdabb3d0000))

**Denmark warns Russia is preparing sabotage against its defense industry.** Denmark's security and intelligence service, PET, said Russia is trying to recruit Danish citizens, sometimes without their knowledge, to photograph defense companies and infrastructure tied to military aid for Ukraine, material PET says could support future sabotage; PET's counterespionage chief cited arson as one possible method and said the service has not confirmed any sabotage actually carried out on Danish soil. *Why it matters:* It fits a wider pattern of Russian pressure on European states supplying Ukraine, following the attempted drone attack at Leipzig airport in Germany.
([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/russia-prepare-sabotage-against-ukraine-linked-danish-companies-denmark-says/), [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/09/03/russia-recruits-danes-for-sabotage-planning-denmark-says))

**China opens new military training center in Laos.** Satellite imagery analyzed by Reuters shows China has built a joint support and training center with a paved runway and an aviation academy at Ban Keun, about 50 kilometers north of Vientiane, on a site that includes part of a former US and CIA airfield from the Vietnam War era. Beijing says the facility will train air force pilots and is not aimed at any third country, but analysts describe it as a logistics hub resembling China's other overseas bases. *Why it matters:* It extends China's military reach in Southeast Asia and gives it a forward logistics option near the South China Sea.
([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/09/china-opens-training-center-with-laos-marking-third-overseas-military-base/), [China Global South](https://chinaglobalsouth.com/2026/09/30/china-expands-military-presence-new-laos-support-center/))

## Germany and Israel

**German intelligence chief warns of growing risk of violent conflict with Russia.** BND president Martin Jaeger told German lawmakers this week that confrontation with Russia has entered a more dangerous phase and that Germany could be drawn into violent conflict, pointing to the attempted drone attack on Leipzig airport in August as an example; US and German authorities have linked that plot, which involved explosives-laden drones aimed at a Ukrainian cargo plane, to Russia's military intelligence service, the GRU. *Why it matters:* It is one of the most direct warnings yet from Germany's own intelligence leadership about the risk of Russian attacks on German soil.
([Irish Examiner](https://www.irishexaminer.com/world/arid-41919964.html), [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/us-intel-links-explosive-drone-found-at-german-airport-to-russias-military-intelligence/))

**Israeli military restricts Palestinian olive harvest access amid settler tensions.** Haaretz reported Israeli soldiers were documented stopping Palestinian landowners from picking olives in the West Bank and threatening to close groves entirely if Israeli and foreign activists accompany the farmers, telling one landowner "you are forbidden to pick olives until the anarchist Jews leave." *Why it matters:* The restrictions add to friction over West Bank access during the olive harvest, a recurring flashpoint between settlers, the military and Palestinian farmers.
([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/west-bank/2026-10-07/ty-article/.premium/idf-threatens-to-close-palestinian-olive-groves-if-activists-accompany-farmers/000001a1-1126-d0df-a3f5-5db634f70000))
