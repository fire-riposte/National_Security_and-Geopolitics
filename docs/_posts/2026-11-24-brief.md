**Bottom line:** China publicly disclosed a rare death sentence for an official convicted of spying for a foreign intelligence service, a sign Beijing is treating insider recruitment as an urgent threat. A US Army sergeant pleaded guilty to selling colleagues' access and classified hard drives to buyers in China, the second of three defendants in the case to admit guilt. In Israel, Mossad's chief is pushing for a strike on Iran over his own analysts' objections, while his predecessor argues publicly that war remains the fastest way to topple Tehran's regime.

## Espionage and counterintelligence

**US Army sergeant pleads guilty to selling military secrets to China.** Sgt. Jian Zhao, a battery supply sergeant at Joint Base Lewis-McChord whose clearance gave him access to sensitive Army material, pleaded guilty in federal court in Portland to conspiring to gather and transfer national defense information to buyers in China. Prosecutors say he was paid $15,000 and sold an encrypted hard drive and 20 classified hard drives to co-conspirators acting for China; he was recruited by Ruoyu Duan, a former soldier set for sentencing December 10. *Why it matters:* it's the latest case of Chinese recruiters working directly through active-duty troops with routine access to sensitive material.
([Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/army-sergeant-pleads-guilty-cashing-024851172.html))

**China publicly discloses death sentence for official convicted of spying.** China's Ministry of State Security said an official surnamed Ma was sentenced to death, stripped of political rights for life, and ordered to forfeit his property after being convicted of passing state secrets to a foreign intelligence service. Officials said he was recruited while working abroad and later sent hundreds of classified documents to his handler in exchange for a monthly salary and bonuses. *Why it matters:* public disclosure of an espionage death sentence is rare for Beijing and reads as a warning about foreign recruitment of officials posted overseas.
([The Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/11/23/china-spy-death-sentence/af82dbac-e788-11f1-8db0-6f1e3c389e7c_story.html), [South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/politics/article/3375436/china-sentences-ministry-official-death-leaking-over-100-state-secrets))

**Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO after Chinese spy scandal.** Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as president and CEO less than two weeks after he stepped down following reports that he had an affair with Alice Huang, a Singapore-based tech executive US officials suspect of passing information to Chinese intelligence. Jensen Huang is not accused of wrongdoing. *Why it matters:* the case shows how personal ties can become an espionage exposure risk at the center of the US-China fight over advanced chips.
([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/tech-news/2026-11-23/ty-article/jensen-huang-reinstated-as-nvidia-ceo-following-brief-resignation-over-scandal/000001a2-08cd-d7ca-abf6-6edda57b0000))

**Hackers breach Austrian Foreign Ministry website.** Austria's Foreign Ministry said hackers breached one of its websites and accessed the names and official contact information of employees at its Vienna headquarters and diplomatic missions abroad. The ministry has not said who is responsible. *Why it matters:* contact details for diplomats are a useful target for follow-on phishing or recruitment attempts by foreign intelligence services.
([The Record](https://therecord.media/austrian-foreign-ministry-says-website-hacked))

**FBI reportedly facing second breach of criminal records database.** The FBI is investigating a second reported data breach involving the National Crime Information Center, the database federal, state and local police use for records on criminals, fugitives and missing people, days after an earlier intrusion came to light. The bureau has not detailed the scope or who is responsible. *Why it matters:* a second reported breach of the same system in one week raises doubts about how well the earlier fix held.
([The Record](https://therecord.media/fbi-cyberattack-data-breach))

## US military and intelligence community

**Navy pilot retention crisis surfaces at Hung Cao's confirmation hearing.** Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao told senators the Navy faces a "historic drop" in fighter pilot retention, with Vice Admiral Jeffrey Czerewko warning of a possible 15 percent or greater drawdown as aviators leave for industry. Cao also faced questions after the Pentagon failed an internal audit. *Why it matters:* losing experienced fighter pilots to the private sector erodes readiness faster than new aircraft can be delivered.
([The Cipher Brief](https://www.thecipherbrief.com/the-navys-problems-come-into-focus-at-hung-caos-confirmation-hearing), [Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/video/2026/11/23/acting-navy-secretary-hung-cao-heads-to-the-hill-and-the-pentagon-fails-an-audit-headlines/))

**Air Force says it posted $16 billion propulsion contract in error.** The Air Force said it still plans to award the $16.1 billion Advanced Propulsion Acquisition Contract but had not yet done so, after the award was posted publicly ahead of schedule. *Why it matters:* the mishap raises questions about controls on one of the service's largest pending acquisition contracts.
([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/propulsion-mega-contract-posted-in-error-air-force-says/))

## China, Russia, Iran and North Korea

**NATO vows to counter Russian nuclear satellite threat.** NATO's chief pledged to defend against the threat after German media reported Russia planned to launch a satellite carrying a nuclear warhead into space in the coming weeks. *Why it matters:* a nuclear detonation in orbit could disable satellites across many countries, not just the target, making it a threat to the whole space domain.
([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c9x2er1ry2mzo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss))

**China's fourth aircraft carrier nears completion.** China's fourth aircraft carrier, known so far as Type 004, is a larger, nuclear-powered vessel that could be unveiled as early as next year, analysts told Defense News. Nuclear power would let it launch aircraft faster than China's existing carriers in a conflict. *Why it matters:* a nuclear-powered carrier would narrow the gap with the US Navy's carrier fleet and extend China's reach well beyond its coastal waters.
([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/global/asia-pacific/2026/11/23/chinas-fourth-aircraft-carrier-bigger-than-others-and-nuclear-powered-nears-completion/))

**US moves to recover $1 billion in crypto stolen by North Korean hackers.** The Justice Department filed an action to forfeit more than $1.1 billion in cryptocurrency that North Korean actors stole in last month's Binance hack, part of a broader effort to help the exchange recover more than $6 billion in stolen assets. *Why it matters:* it's one of the largest publicly disclosed recoveries tied to North Korea's state-sponsored crypto theft, which funds its weapons programs.
([Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/united-states-files-action-recover-more-1-billion-cryptocurrency-assets-stolen-north-korean))

**North Korea increasingly outsources its cybercrime to foreign freelancers.** The Cipher Brief reports North Korea is relying more on non-Korean freelance developers, including Iranian and South African contractors paid a few hundred dollars a month, to run fraudulent IT-worker schemes and other cybercrime once handled by North Koreans directly. *Why it matters:* outsourcing makes the scheme harder to track by nationality alone, complicating sanctions enforcement.
([The Cipher Brief](https://www.thecipherbrief.com/north-korea-is-outsourcing-its-cybercrime-empire-heres-how))

## Germany and Israel

**Mossad chief pushes Iran strike over his own analysts' objections.** Mossad chief Roman Gofman continues to push for military action against Iran in Israeli security forums and in Washington, even as agency analysts say there is no urgent need for a strike, Haaretz reports. The disagreement comes as Prime Minister Netanyahu weighs renewed strikes. *Why it matters:* a public rift between an intelligence chief and his own analysts undercuts the credibility of whichever assessment the government acts on.
([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/israel-security/2026-11-23/ty-article/.premium/mossad-analysts-oppose-iran-strike-as-netanyahu-loyalist-chief-pushes-for-attack/000001a2-0952-d7ca-abf6-6f5e61c60000))

**Former Mossad chief says war remains the fallback to topple Iran's regime.** David Barnea, who led Mossad until this year, said Iran will keep pursuing nuclear weapons as long as its current regime survives, and proposed a full blockade of the Strait of Hormuz alongside continued sanctions as an alternative to another war, warning that war remains the fastest route to regime change if those measures fail. *Why it matters:* a former intelligence chief making the public case for war, not just deterrence, adds pressure on Israel's current Iran deliberations.
([Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/mossad-chief-warns-iran-still-122324075.html))

**Bundeswehr tests kamikaze drones on NATO's northeastern flank.** Germany's armed forces tested the tactical deployment of loitering munitions, commonly called kamikaze drones, during drills on NATO's northeastern flank, following earlier decisions to build attritable strike-drone capability. *Why it matters:* it's a concrete step in fielding the drone-warfare capability Germany decided to build less than two years ago, now tested in the forward posture area it would matter most.
([Augen geradeaus!, in German](https://augengeradeaus.net/2026/11/bundeswehr-testet-taktischen-einsatz-von-kamikaze-drohnen-an-der-nato-nordostflanke/))

**IDF investigating use of Palestinians as human shields in West Bank.** Haaretz reports the Israeli military is investigating the use of Palestinians as human shields in the West Bank, a practice it says is widely used by soldiers in Gaza and officially banned by the IDF but has gone uninvestigated in the West Bank until now; Haaretz says a person was killed in a related incident in Lebanon. *Why it matters:* the practice is a war crime under international law, and the gap between the Gaza and West Bank response points to uneven internal enforcement.
([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/israel-security/2026-11-24/ty-article/.premium/israeli-military-probing-use-of-palestinians-as-human-shields-in-the-west-bank/000001a2-0a3b-d0d9-a9f6-befbaddd0000))
