**Bottom line:** The CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) confirmed hackers breached an unclassified open-source intelligence system, days after Huawei was convicted in US federal court on Iran sanctions, bank fraud and trade secret theft charges. German intelligence is warning Russia could put a nuclear-armed satellite into orbit by year's end, and the Bundeswehr says it's readying jamming and other countermeasures. In Israel, Mossad's own analysts are reportedly resisting their chief's push for a renewed strike on Iran.

## Espionage and counterintelligence

**Hackers breach CIA and ODNI open-source intelligence system.** The CIA and ODNI confirmed hackers broke into an unclassified open-source intelligence (OSINT) analysis system, after the New York Times first reported the intrusion; a source told CyberScoop "no other systems are currently at risk." A cybersecurity researcher said the breach still matters because seeing what analysts search for can expose US intelligence priorities and blind spots. Several outlets, citing the Times' reporting, said China was suspected and that the hackers used an AI-assisted tool, though CyberScoop's own sourcing did not confirm either detail. *Why it matters:* even a breach limited to unclassified systems can reveal what US spy agencies are watching, and whom.
([CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/cia-odni-osint-data-breach/))

**Army sergeant at Joint Base Lewis-McChord pleads guilty in China-linked espionage case.** Jian Zhao, 37, an active-duty US Army sergeant stationed at Joint Base Lewis-McChord in Washington state, pleaded guilty to conspiring to gather, transmit and transfer national defense information to individuals connected to China, the Justice Department said. *Why it matters:* it's another case of an active-duty service member admitting to passing US defense information to parties tied to Beijing.
([Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/active-duty-us-army-sergeant-pleads-guilty-conspiracy-gather-and-transfer-national-defense))

**Huawei convicted of Iran sanctions violations, trade secret theft and fraud.** A federal jury in the Eastern District of New York convicted Huawei Technologies and three subsidiaries after a three-month trial on charges including violating US sanctions on Iran, lying to banks, money laundering and stealing trade secrets. *Why it matters:* the verdict adds a criminal conviction to Washington's long-running case that Huawei's global business doubles as a vehicle for sanctions evasion and industrial espionage.
([Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/chinese-tech-giant-huawei-and-subsidiaries-convicted-sanctions-violations-fraud-conspiracy))

**China sentences ministry official to death for espionage, state media says.** Chinese state-linked media reported that a ministry employee identified only as Ma was sentenced to death for passing more than 100 pieces of information, including dozens of classified state secrets, to a foreign intelligence service in exchange for more than 1 million yuan. The disclosure came through Gold Wall Press, tied to China's state secrets watchdog, and basic details, including which ministry Ma worked for and whether the sentence has been carried out, were not disclosed. *Why it matters:* China rarely publicizes death sentences for espionage, and a state-media account of the case can't be independently verified.
([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/china-spy-sentence-death-ma/))

**MI5 warns UK defense firms of Russian sabotage risk.** Britain's domestic intelligence agency, MI5, issued an alert urging defense industry firms to tighten security immediately, warning of the risk of hostile reconnaissance and attacks on their people and sites. *Why it matters:* the warning signals UK security services see a live, near-term sabotage threat to the defense industrial base, not just a cyber one.
([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/uks-mi5-warns-local-defense-firms-of-russian-sabotage-risk/))

## US military and intelligence community

**FBI stays quiet on second crime database hack this year.** FBI Director Kash Patel declined to discuss a breach of the National Crime Information Center, citing an ongoing investigation; it's the second breach of an FBI database in recent months. *Why it matters:* repeated breaches of a system police nationwide rely on for criminal records raise questions about the bureau's own cybersecurity that Patel has yet to address publicly.
([CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/kash-patel-fbi-ncic-database-hack/))

**Navy pilot retention crisis surfaces at Hung Cao confirmation hearing.** At a Senate hearing on Hung Cao's nomination to be undersecretary of the Navy, officials testified the Navy faces "a pretty historic drop in retention" of advanced fighter pilots, with a possible drawdown of 15 percent or more as pilots leave for industry. *Why it matters:* losing experienced fighter pilots faster than the Navy can train replacements erodes a hard-to-rebuild capability just as the service faces rising demands in the Pacific.
([The Cipher Brief](https://www.thecipherbrief.com/the-navys-problems-come-into-focus-at-hung-caos-confirmation-hearing))

**White House seeks funding carve-outs for Golden Dome and a proposed battleship.** The White House is asking for funding exemptions for the Golden Dome missile defense program and a Trump-ordered battleship in the next government funding stopgap, warning the Pentagon's munitions ramp-up could stall if other program funding anomalies aren't approved too. *Why it matters:* carving out favored programs from a stopgap funding bill means other Pentagon priorities compete for whatever funding is left.
([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/white-house-seeks-funding-exemptions-for-golden-dome-trump-battleship-in-next-funding-stopgap/))

## China, Russia, Iran and North Korea

**Russia's own data show nearly 500,000 dead in Ukraine war.** Leaked Russian government data show the war in Ukraine has reshaped mortality patterns inside Russia, with the country's own figures putting its death toll from the invasion at almost 500,000. *Why it matters:* a toll of that size, if it holds up, shows the war's human cost to Russia is far higher than Moscow has publicly acknowledged.
([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmx27g533zwyo))

**South Korea scrambles jets as Russian bombers approach its airspace.** South Korea scrambled F-15K fighters and issued a shelter order for residents after Russian Tu-160 bombers and fighter escorts came within seconds of crossing into South Korean airspace. *Why it matters:* a near-incursion by Russian strategic bombers near South Korea signals Moscow is willing to probe US allies' airspace far from Ukraine.
([The War Zone](https://www.twz.com/air/seoul-scrambles-f-15ks-as-russian-warplanes-come-within-seconds-of-crossing-into-south-korean-airspace))

## Germany and Israel

**German intelligence warns of Russian nuclear satellite; Bundeswehr preps countermeasures.** German public media, citing intelligence sources, reported Russia may put a satellite carrying a nuclear weapon into orbit by the end of the year. The Bundeswehr's Space Command commander, Michael Traut, said a Russian nuclear attack on satellites is plausible and that the military is preparing jamming, blinding and other counter-space measures. *Why it matters:* a single nuclear detonation in low orbit could disable large parts of the civilian and military satellite infrastructure the Bundeswehr itself increasingly depends on.
([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/global/europe/2026/11/24/russia-may-launch-nuke-laden-satellite-by-years-end-german-report-warns/), [ZDFheute, in German](https://www.zdfheute.de/politik/deutschland/bundeswehr-satellit-weltraum-krieg-russland-100.html))

**New York Times: Mossad abducted and killed Iranian general in 2006 Istanbul operation.** The New York Times, citing former US officials, reported Mossad agents abducted Iranian general Alireza Asgari from an Istanbul hotel in December 2006, interrogated him for weeks over the fate of missing Israeli airman Ron Arad, then killed him and dumped his body in the Black Sea. Asgari's wife told the paper Iranian investigators concluded Israeli agents drugged and abducted him; Israel has previously denied involvement, and the allegations remain unconfirmed. *Why it matters:* the account, if accurate, is a rare detailed look at a two-decade-old Mossad operation Israel has never acknowledged.
([Ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/j7kixs6gy))

**Berlin bans Hezbollah-linked association.** Berlin's state interior department banned the El-Irschad association and three affiliated groups on November 17, designating them fronts for Hezbollah's civilian activity in Germany. *Why it matters:* it's part of a wider German crackdown on networks that fund or support Hezbollah from outside Lebanon.
([Alma Center](https://israel-alma.org/the-el-irschad-association-in-berlin-and-hezbollahs-civilian-activities-outside-lebanon/))

**Mossad analysts push back on chief's call for renewed Iran strike.** Haaretz reports an ongoing rift inside Mossad, with chief Roman Gofman continuing to press for military action against Iran in Israeli security forums and in Washington, while agency officials say there is no urgent need for a strike. *Why it matters:* an open split between an intelligence chief and his own analysts over the case for war is a real check that could slow any rush to action.
([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/israel-security/2026-11-23/ty-article/.premium/mossad-analysts-oppose-iran-strike-as-netanyahu-loyalist-chief-pushes-for-attack/000001a2-0952-d7ca-abf6-6f5e61c60000))
