**Bottom line:** Western intelligence agencies say Russia is plotting to assassinate European defense-industry executives, and Germany's BND chief told the Bundestag Russia is waging a "shadow war" against the country, just as a former BND president was arrested on treason and espionage charges. The FBI and Justice Department notched a run of China-related counterintelligence actions, including a takedown of Flax Typhoon hacking infrastructure and the arrest of a California woman accused of spying on Taiwan's president's son. Trump ruled out striking Iran before the midterms as the EU adopted its largest Russia sanctions package since 2022 and Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia escalated.

## Espionage and counterintelligence

**Russia is plotting to assassinate European defense executives, Western intelligence agencies warn.** Western spy agencies have told European governments that Russian intelligence services are planning to assassinate executives and officials in the defense industry, the Financial Times reported. The warning lands alongside other reporting on a broader Russian campaign of sabotage and recruitment targeting the continent. *Why it matters:* A campaign of targeted killings against defense-industry leaders would mark a sharp escalation beyond the sabotage and cyberattacks Russia has already been linked to in Europe.
([Financial Times via archive.is](https://archive.is/20261011102848/https://www.ft.com/content/5da2dd05-9583-4a75-b018-2c5024383ec4))

**FBI, CISA and NSA expose Chinese contractor behind years of Flax Typhoon hacking.** US agencies, working with partners in Australia, Japan, the UK, Spain, New Zealand and Canada, published a 58-page advisory detailing six years of hacking tools linked to Integrity Technology Group, a Beijing company Western officials say China's Ministry of State Security hired to support attacks on universities, government agencies, telecoms and media worldwide. The FBI and Justice Department seized websites supporting two of the group's tools, including one that had given remote access to roughly 20 Taiwanese universities, while other campaign tools hit a South Carolina power company and airports in Japan and Poland. *Why it matters:* The advisory shows Chinese state-linked hackers had sustained access to critical infrastructure and research institutions across multiple US allies for years before being disrupted.
([The Record](https://therecord.media/flax-typhoon-china-tools-integrity-tech-international-takedown))

**California woman charged as unregistered Chinese agent, accused of surveilling Taiwan president's son.** Federal prosecutors charged Wanying "Heather" Zhang, 34, of Irvine, with acting as an unregistered agent of China after she was arrested at Los Angeles International Airport on October 4 while trying to board a flight to Shanghai. A criminal complaint alleges she worked with an associate to photograph and record a family outside their Seattle-area home; CBS News, citing law enforcement sources, identified the target as Ting-Yu Lai, the US-based son of Taiwanese President Lai Ching-te, though the complaint itself does not name him. *Why it matters:* FBI Director Kash Patel cited the arrest as a warning to foreign adversaries running espionage operations on US soil.
([KTVU](https://www.ktvu.com/news/california-realtor-nabbed-lax-alleged-china-spy-case-targeting-taiwan-presidents-relative))

**Canadian cybersecurity executive arrested in FBI extortion case tied to ShinyHunters.** The FBI arrested Edward Dubrovsky, an executive at Canadian ransomware-negotiation firm Cypfer, on federal extortion charges. Investigators say the case aligns with their probe into the ShinyHunters hacking group's breach of the FBI's own IT systems. *Why it matters:* The arrest suggests investigators believe insiders in the ransomware-negotiation industry may have helped extort victims rather than just helping them respond.
([CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/edward-dubrovsky-cypfer-arrested-fbi-extortion-charges/))

**MI5 warning over Chinese-linked research institute reaches Scottish universities.** MI5 has told UK universities, including in Scotland, to review ties with the China General Technology Research Institute, saying it has "very strong" ties to China's Ministry of State Security and funds research that could aid Chinese espionage in AI, cybersecurity and covert communications. More than 100 UK-linked academics have worked on institute-funded projects, some without knowing who was ultimately paying, and the UK's security minister has written to university leaders asking them to end the arrangements. *Why it matters:* The warning shows how adversary states can embed espionage-relevant research funding inside ordinary academic collaboration.
([The Scotsman](https://www.scotsman.com/education/scots-universities-warned-over-chinese-and-russian-spies-9278640))

## US military and intelligence community

**Army activates new multi-domain task force for the Indo-Pacific.** The Army activated its 4th Multi-Domain Task Force, which includes four battalions and is planned to grow to 2,100 soldiers, to "deny" and "disrupt" adversaries in the Indo-Pacific. *Why it matters:* The task force adds a dedicated long-range strike and sensing capability in the region where competition with China is most acute.
([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/news/your-military/2026/10/09/us-army-activates-new-multi-domain-task-force-to-deny-disrupt-enemies-in-indo-pacific/))

**Army revives "night court" review to redirect funding toward priorities.** The Army restarted its "night court" audit process this summer, shifting more than $100 million out of legacy programs, with the initial effort focused on science and technology spending. *Why it matters:* The review signals the Army is still willing to force trade-offs between modernization priorities and existing programs even as overall budgets grow.
([Defense One](https://www.defenseone.com/policy/2026/10/army-brings-back-night-court-audit-programs-shifting-more-100m/416547/))

## China, Russia, Iran and North Korea

**EU adopts largest Russia sanctions package since 2022.** EU foreign ministers were set to formally adopt a new sanctions package in Luxembourg on October 12 after ambassadors approved it on October 7, adding close to 1,600 individuals and entities tied to Russia's military-industrial complex, more than half of them linked to missile production, to the bloc's asset-freeze and travel-ban lists. *Why it matters:* It is the largest single batch of Russia-related sanctions listings since the 2022 invasion, though it stops short of the sweeping new energy and financial measures some member states wanted.
([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/eu-ambassadors-wave-through-largest-ever-list-of-russia-sanctions-2/))

**Trump rules out striking Iran before the midterms, keeps naval blockade.** President Trump said on October 8 the US will not attack Iran before the November 3 midterm elections, while keeping in place the naval blockade imposed earlier in the conflict. Axios reported that US Central Command had separately been told to complete preparations for a possible resumption of major combat operations, though no date has been set. *Why it matters:* The pledge buys time for diplomacy over the Strait of Hormuz standoff but leaves open a resumption of strikes once the election has passed.
([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/08/trump-iran-strikes-midterm-elections))

**Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia escalate, Riyadh airport hit twice.** Yemen's Houthi movement struck Riyadh's international airport on back-to-back days, killing at least 13 people in the first attack and one more in a second strike, prompting a Saudi vow of a "serious response." Iran, the Houthis' main backer, offered to mediate the broader Saudi-Yemen conflict even as its foreign minister said the region's crises have no military solution. *Why it matters:* The escalation raises the risk of a wider regional conflict that could draw in the US and Iran just as Washington tries to avoid new entanglements before the midterms.
([BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cjly0zqq27e4o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss), [Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/middle-east-news/2026-10-11/ty-article/after-houthi-threat-attack-at-saudi-airport-sparks-fire-saudi-coalition-says/000001a1-2b90-d1b4-a5fb-afdcc7bb0000))

**Philippine-Chinese incidents spike in South China Sea.** Incidents between Chinese and Philippine forces have spiked in recent weeks across the South China Sea and Luzon Strait, with small boats, cutters and aircraft from both sides engaging dangerously at least several times as each country presses its territorial claims. *Why it matters:* The uptick raises the risk of a miscalculation drawing in the US, which has a mutual defense treaty with the Philippines.
([USNI News](https://news.usni.org/2026/10/09/philippine-and-chinese-incidents-spike-in-south-china-sea-luzon-strait))

## Germany and Israel

**Former BND chief arrested on treason and espionage allegations.** German federal prosecutors arrested August Hanning, 80, who led the BND foreign intelligence service from 1998 to 2005, along with a former BND chief of staff identified as Manfred D., on suspicion of "treasonous espionage, spying out state secrets, attempted treason and espionage." Investigators allege Hanning began buying classified intelligence from a former BND official in 2010 and used it to prepare an analysis for an employee of a foreign intelligence service; prosecutors have not named the country, though Der Spiegel reported investigators are examining a possible link to Azerbaijan. The arrest comes days after BND President Martin Jäger told a Bundestag oversight hearing that Russia is waging a "shadow war" against Germany, citing the foiled Leipzig drone-sabotage plot. *Why it matters:* An arrest of this seniority is unprecedented for German intelligence and raises questions about oversight of a service meant to counter exactly this kind of infiltration.
([EUobserver](https://euobserver.com/241332/german-ex-spy-chief-arrested-over-espionage-and-treason-allegations-involving-leaked-bnd-files/), [ZDF, in German](https://www.zdfheute.de/politik/nachrichtendienste-anhoerung-bnd-russland-bedrohung-100.html))

**IDF kills Hamas Nukhba commander from the October 7 attack.** The Israeli military said it killed Ahmed Hassan Salameh a-Swarkeh, a Nukhba force squad commander it says invaded Israeli communities on October 7 and later carried out sniper attacks on Israeli troops in Beit Hanoun, northern Gaza. Troops from the 414th Combat Intelligence Collection Unit tracked him before an air force drone strike killed him; the IDF said steps were taken to avoid harming civilians. *Why it matters:* Israel continues to prioritize killing or capturing October 7 participants more than two years after the attack.
([The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog_entry/idf-nukhba-force-squad-commander-who-invaded-israel-on-october-7-killed-in-airstrike/))

**Israeli intelligence soldiers describe shift in Gaza targeting policy.** Intelligence and drone personnel told Haaretz that rules for approving strikes loosened over time, with strikes approved despite the risk of hundreds of civilian casualties and orders not to question assigned missions, including for low-ranking targets. The IDF disputes the soldiers' account of its strike-approval policy. *Why it matters:* The soldiers' testimony, if accurate, points to a systemic loosening of safeguards rather than isolated incidents.
([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/israel-security/2026-10-12/ty-article-magazine/.premium/even-the-lowest-ranking-target-justified-civilian-casualties-israeli-intel-soldiers-describe-shift-in-targeting-policy-in-gaza/000001a1-2ab9-d02f-a5bb-6fff7e540000))

**IDF officials warn pre-election Iran strike could look politically motivated.** Israeli military officials acknowledged Iran has replenished much of its ballistic missile stockpile but warned that a strike on the eve of Israel's election could be seen as politically motivated and further erode public trust. *Why it matters:* The officials' caution suggests military planners want any future confrontation with Iran insulated from domestic political timing.
([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/israel-security/2026-10-10/ty-article/.premium/idf-officials-warn-pre-election-iran-strike-could-seem-politically-motivated/000001a1-2711-d5af-a3b7-2fb18fe70000))
